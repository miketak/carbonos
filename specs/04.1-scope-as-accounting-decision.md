# 04.1: Scope as an accounting decision

- **Status**: Draft
- **Protocol**: Chapter 4 (scope is determined by the reporter's relationship
  to the source), Appendix F (leased assets), the Scope 3 Standard's fifteen
  categories
- **Owner**: Michael Takrama
- **Created**: 2026-09-02
- **Modules**: `ghg`, `src/features/ghg`

## Problem

Spec 04 fixes scope by emission factor. The Standard fixes it by
*relationship*: the same physics lands in different scopes depending on who
owns or controls the source. Diesel burned in the company's own haul fleet is
scope 1; the same diesel burned by a mining contractor's fleet on the same pit
is scope 3. Electricity the company generates and consumes is scope 1 (the fuel
is), purchased electricity is scope 2. A mining client cannot classify
contractor mining, one of its largest sources, at all. And two structural gaps
in the category list: no PROCESS_EMISSIONS kind for scope 1 (explosives, lime,
cement, coal-seam methane), and four of the fifteen scope 3 categories.

## Behaviour

- Classification records **an emission factor and a scope/category**, both
  chosen by the accountant. The library offers scope-agnostic factors where
  the physics is the same (one "Diesel" factor usable in scope 1 or scope 3)
  with a suggested default; factors whose scope is inherent (grid electricity)
  keep it.
- `ActivityCategory` gains `PROCESS_EMISSIONS` (scope 1) and the scope 3
  categories needed for the Standard's fifteen (purchased goods and services,
  capital goods, fuel- and energy-related, upstream and downstream transport,
  waste, business travel, commuting, leased assets up and down, processing and
  use of sold products, end of life, franchises, investments).
- **Leased assets** per Appendix F: an assignment may carry a lease type. Under
  operational control, assets the company leases *in* and operates are scope 1
  or 2 (finance and operating leases alike); under equity share or financial
  control, finance leases are scope 1 or 2 and operating leases scope 3
  (upstream leased assets). Assets leased *out* mirror this downstream. The
  derivation applies the inventory's approach.
- The CLASSIFICATION gate errors when the chosen scope and factor are
  incompatible (a grid factor in scope 1) and warns when the scope differs from
  the factor's suggested default, so deliberate choices are visible.

Given the QA scenario extended with "Contractor mining fleet diesel, 2,100,000
litre" at Obuasi: classified with the Diesel factor and scope 3, category
"Upstream transportation" or, better, a purchased-services category, it lands
in scope 3 at 5,586 t rather than being unclassifiable.

## API

`PUT /assignments/{id}/classify` takes `{emissionFactorId, scope, category,
leaseType?}`; `EmissionFactorResponse` gains `defaultScope` and
`scopeAgnostic`; assignment and run-line responses already carry scope and
category.

## Data

`ghg_assignments` and `ghg_run_lines` keep `scope` and `category` but they are
now set from the request, not copied from the factor; a `lease_type` column on
assignments and lines; factor rows for the new categories and the
process-emission factors (ANFO, lime) via migration.

## Events

None.

## Verification

Integration tests: the same factor in scope 1 and scope 3 in two assignments
yields the right scope totals; incompatible scope refused; leased asset scope
under each approach; the ANFO record classifies as a process emission.

## Non-goals and open questions

Automatic scope inference from facility or supplier metadata. Whether the
seeded factors stay scope-tagged with a default or become fully agnostic
(recommendation: tagged with a default, agnostic where the physics allows).
