# 04.1: Scope as an accounting decision

- **Status**: Implemented
- **Protocol**: Chapter 4 (scope is determined by the reporter's relationship
  to the source), the separately published Appendix F (Categorizing GHG
  Emissions from Leased Assets, 2004), the Scope 3 Standard's fifteen
  categories
- **Owner**: Michael Takrama
- **Created**: 2026-09-02; implemented 2026-09-08
- **Modules**: `ghg` (`ActivityCategory`, `LeaseType`, classification,
  CLASSIFICATION gate), `src/features/ghg` (classify controls, factor library)

## Problem

Spec 04 fixed scope by emission factor. The Standard fixes it by
*relationship*: the same physics lands in different scopes depending on who
owns or controls the source. Diesel burned in the company's own haul fleet is
scope 1; the same diesel burned by a mining contractor's fleet on the same pit
is scope 3. A mining client could not classify contractor mining, one of its
largest sources, at all. And two structural gaps in the category list: no
process-emissions kind for scope 1 (explosives, lime, cement, coal-seam
methane), and four of the fifteen scope 3 categories.

## Behavior

- Classification records **an emission factor and a scope/category**, both
  chosen by the accountant. Absent, they default from the factor. A factor is
  either **scope-agnostic** (the seeded fuels: natural gas, liquefied petroleum gas, diesel,
  petrol, the process-emission factors and biomass), usable in scope 1 or
  scope 3 with a suggested default, or its scope is inherent (grid
  electricity, district heat, the scope 3 activity factors).
- `ActivityCategory` covers the Standard's scope 1 kinds (stationary
  combustion, mobile combustion, **process emissions**, fugitive emissions),
  the scope 2 kinds, and the Scope 3 Standard's fifteen categories:
  purchased goods and services, capital goods, fuel- and energy-related,
  upstream transport, waste, business travel, employee commuting, upstream
  leased assets, downstream transport, processing of sold products, use of
  sold products, end of life of sold products, downstream leased assets,
  franchises, investments. Each category belongs to one scope; a category from
  another scope is refused with 409 at classification.
- **Leased assets** per Appendix F: a classification may carry a lease type
  (`FINANCE_LEASE_IN`, `OPERATING_LEASE_IN`, `FINANCE_LEASE_OUT`,
  `OPERATING_LEASE_OUT`), and then the scope and category derive from the lease
  type under the inventory's approach (`LeaseType.derive`). Under operational
  control, assets leased *in* are scope 1 or 2 whatever the lease type; under
  equity share or financial control, finance leases are scope 1 or 2 and
  operating leases scope 3 (upstream leased assets). Assets leased *out* mirror
  this downstream: a finance lease out is always scope 3 (downstream leased
  assets); an operating lease out is scope 3 under operational control and
  scope 1 or 2 otherwise. The derivation assumes the lessee operates what it
  leases in, which Chapter 4 describes as the normal case. A leased-in asset
  the company does not operate is scope 3 under operational control and must
  be classified by hand.
- The CLASSIFICATION gate errors when the chosen scope departs from the
  default (the stream's, or the factor's) without a recorded justification,
  and is silent once one is recorded or when a lease type derived the scope
  (spec 04.3). No factor's scope is inherent.
- The library gains **process-emission factors** (ammonium nitrate fuel oil (ANFO) explosives detonation,
  quicklime calcination) so a mining client's explosives classify as a scope 1
  process emission.

Given "Contractor mining fleet diesel, 2,000 litre" at Obuasi beside the
company's own 1,000 litre: the same Diesel factor classified in scope 3 under
purchased goods and services yields scope 1 = 2,660 kg and scope 3 = 5,320 kg.

## API

`PUT /assignments/{id}/classify` takes `{emissionFactorId, scope?, category?,
leaseType?}`; 409 when the category belongs to another scope or a scope has no
category. `EmissionFactorResponse` carries `defaultScope`, `defaultCategory`
and `scopeAgnostic` (instead of `scope` and `category`). Assignment and
run-line responses carry `scope`, `category` and `leaseType`.
