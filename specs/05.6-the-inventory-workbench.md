# 05.6: The inventory as a workbench

- **Status**: Implemented (2026-09-16)
- **Protocol**: Chapter 7 (inventory quality management: the review a
  preparer and a reviewer actually perform), Chapter 9 (a report traceable
  to its records), ISO 14064-1:2018 section 8.2 (GHG information management
  and records)
- **Owner**: Michael Takrama
- **Created**: 2026-09-16
- **Modules**: `src/features/ghg` (`InventoryDetailPage`,
  `inventoryFilters`, `AssignmentsSection`, `AssignmentDrawer`,
  `PreflightBanner`). No backend change.

## Problem

The inventory page mounted seven section components on one scroll, as
equal-weight sibling cards: identity and lineage, the lifecycle bar, the
organizational and operational boundary, the activity view, the upstream
rules and market instruments, the launch and its runs, the report metadata,
and the history. About 3,800 lines of interface arrived at once, and they
serve seven different jobs at seven different frequencies. The lineage block
is read once. The boundary is set once and then left alone. The report
header is filled in at publication. The activity view is where a preparer
spends the working day, and it sat in the middle with one card above it and
three below.

Inside the activity view the same problem repeated in a worse form. Spec
05.5 removed the 107-option factor select from the row, but the editor
stayed there: `ClassifyControls` rendered the factor picker with its own
search and paging, the density, the scope, the category, the lease type, the
proxy flag and two justification fields **inside every row**, 448 lines of
it, fifty times a page. The row could not be read at a glance because most
of it was a form.

None of the view's state was addressable. The search, the seven filters and
the page were `useState`, so a reload lost them, the back button left the
page, and a reviewer could not send a colleague a link to the twelve records
that block the freeze.

The activity register already went through this (spec 04.6). This spec
applies the same pattern to the inventory.

## Behavior

- **Five tabs, in the order the work happens.** `Records` (the default),
  `Boundary`, `Method`, `Runs`, `Report`. This is also how the QA
  procedures divide the page: 004 is the boundary and the lifecycle, 005 is
  classification and the pre-flight, 006 is the market instruments, 007 is
  runs and reports. `Boundary` carries the number of facilities in the
  boundary and `Runs` the number of runs; the others carry no count,
  because a count of cards means nothing.
- **The header and the lifecycle stay above the tabs.** The name, the
  approach, the status, the GWP set, the period and `Edit inventory` are the
  page's identity, and the lifecycle bar is the act every tab depends on.
  The lineage (what this inventory inherited, what it superseded, what a
  published snapshot warns about) collapses behind a disclosure: it is read
  once and it was taking the top third of the first screen.
- **The pre-flight is a banner above the tabs.** It says whether a run may
  launch, names the gates that block it, counts the records that would stop
  a freeze, and offers one action that switches to the unclassified records.
  It is above the tabs because what blocks a freeze matters whichever tab
  you are on. The full findings list stays in `PreflightPanel`, under the
  register on the Records tab, beside the records that fail the gates.
- **The view is the URL.** `tab`, `q`, `facility`, `status`, `scope`,
  `category`, `stream`, `lease`, `page` and `record` are query parameters. A
  default is elided, so a plain inventory link is still clean. Any change to
  what is listed resets `page`. A link reopens the same view; a reload keeps
  the filter a reviewer was working under; the back button steps back
  through the view rather than off the page.
- **A row is one line.** Fact, facility and period, quantity, the chosen
  factor **as text** with its packs and its approval mark, and the status
  pills. A record with no factor says so; a record with a suggestion names
  it. Clicking the row opens it.
- **The editor is a drawer.** `?record=<id>` opens a non-modal panel on the
  right, keyed by the record so the form resets without an effect, taking
  the current page so previous and next need no fetch. Two tabs inside:
  **Classify** (the picker, the density, the scope and category, the lease
  type, the proxy flag, the justifications) and **Exclude** (the reason,
  then the justification and the magnitude, spec 04.4 and 04.8). An excluded
  record has no tabs: it states its decision and offers the way back in. A
  reader who may not write sees the facts and no controls (spec 01.4).
  Classification saves on every change, as it always has: there is no Save
  button, because each control is one accounting decision the server accepts
  or refuses on its own.
- **A page of records can be excluded at once.** Ticking rows offers
  `Exclude n selected`, which takes one reason and one justification. The
  magnitude is deliberately **not** asked for: Chapter 9 wants a size per
  record and one number typed once cannot be it, so each record is recorded
  as not estimated unless the preparer states that the records emit nothing
  (spec 04.8). A record a published run already counted is left in place and
  named in the result, as the register's bulk remove does.
- **Keyboard.** `/` focuses the search, `j` and `k` (and the arrow keys)
  move a cursor through the page, `Enter` opens the record under the cursor.
  The cursor follows the open record and never points outside the page.
  Shortcuts are off while a modal is open.

## API

None. Every endpoint this page uses already exists: `GET
/inventories/{id}/assignments` with the search and filters (spec 05.5),
`PUT /assignments/{id}/classification`, `POST /assignments/{id}/exclude`,
`POST /assignments/{id}/include`, and the emission factor search of spec
02.3.

## Verification

- `inventoryFilters.test.tsx`: defaults elided from the URL, unknown values
  clamped, any list change resetting `page`, and the outgoing query object.
- `AssignmentDrawer.test.tsx`: the drawer opens on the record named in the
  URL and states its place in the page; a record the filters left behind
  says so; the picker cites each publication and hides unapproved rows; it
  asks the server for its page and tells same-named factors apart;
  classification sends the factor with its default scope and category; a
  departing scope is shown and asks for its justification; the unit
  conversion and the density; the grid suggestion and an inherited lease;
  the three exclusion magnitudes and the Montreal Protocol gas; a verifier
  reads and cannot write; an excluded record offers the way back in.
- `InventoryDetailPage.test.tsx`: the workbench opens on the records with
  the banner above the tabs; each tab holds what it should; a row reads as
  one line and opens the drawer; the filters reach the server; a page of
  records is excluded under one reason.
- QA procedures 004, 005, 006 and 007 navigate this page and name the tab
  each step is on.

The suite is also the evidence that the restructure worked:
`InventoryDetailPage.test.tsx` carried `vi.setConfig({ testTimeout: 30000 })`
because rendering the whole page on every test was too slow. With the
sections behind tabs and the classification tests in their own file, that
override is gone.

## Non-goals and open questions

- **Saved intensity metrics do not come back.** `ReportMetadataCard` starts
  from an empty set on every load, so a save rewrites the denominators
  rather than editing them. `InventoryResponse` does not carry them: only
  `ReportMetadataRequest` writes them and `ReportResponse` reads them, and
  `InventoryService.intensityMetrics(UUID)` is not exposed on any GET.
  Fixing it is a backend change and belongs to its own spec.
- Bulk **classification** is not offered. A factor is chosen against one
  record's unit and one record's stream; applying it across a selection
  would need the conversion and the scope resolved per record first.
- The mobile card layout of the old view is gone. The register is a table
  that scrolls horizontally on a narrow screen, as the activity register is.
