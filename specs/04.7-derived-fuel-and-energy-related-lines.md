# 04.7: Derived fuel- and energy-related lines, and the scope select unlocked

- **Status**: Approved (2026-09-12, GHG officer review)
- **Protocol**: Scope 3 Standard category 3 (fuel- and energy-related
  activities not included in scope 1 or scope 2: upstream emissions of
  purchased fuels, upstream emissions of purchased electricity, transmission
  and distribution losses), Scope 3 Standard chapter 6 (minimum boundary),
  Corporate Standard chapter 4 (scope follows the reporter's relationship to
  the source), Appendix F
- **Owner**: Michael Takrama
- **Created**: 2026-09-12 (audit 2026-09-11, findings F41 and F34)
- **Modules**: `ghg` (`UpstreamRule`, the run, the CLASSIFICATION gate, the
  scope 3 cross-check, run lines, `RunCsv`, the report), `src/features/ghg`
  (inventory page: upstream rules card; activity view: classify controls;
  run page and report)

## Problem

A record takes one factor and produces one line. Category 3 of the Scope 3
Standard is made of lines that ride on records that already exist: the
well-to-tank emissions of every litre of diesel in scope 1, and the upstream
and transmission-loss emissions of every kilowatt-hour in scope 2. The only
way to quantify them was to enter every fuel and electricity record a second
time under another activity type, which doubles the register, breaks the
coverage matrix, and leaves two facts where there is one. The Asante report
printed "3. Fuel- and energy-related activities: declared, not quantified:
no reason recorded" although the well-to-tank and Ghana transmission-loss
factors had been imported. For a Ghanaian mine well-to-tank diesel is 10 to
20 percent of scope 1 diesel and transmission losses are about 20 percent of
scope 2. Both are material, and both are in the category's minimum boundary.

Separately, the scope select on the activity view is disabled for a factor
that is not scope-agnostic, with the text "This factor's scope is inherent."
Specs 04.1 and 04.3 both state that the inherent-scope lock is gone and that
any factor can be used in any scope with a justification; the API accepts
such a classification. The lock survives only in the screen. An on-site
landfill is scope 1 methane, and electricity consumed by a tenant at an
asset leased out is category 13; a preparer cannot record either.

## Behavior

### Upstream rules

- An inventory holds **upstream rules**. A rule names a **primary factor**,
  an **upstream factor**, and a **kind**: `WELL_TO_TANK` or
  `TRANSMISSION_AND_DISTRIBUTION`. The upstream factor's unit must convert
  from the primary factor's unit (a well-to-tank diesel factor per litre for
  a diesel factor per litre; a transmission-loss factor per kWh for a grid
  factor per kWh); the API refuses a pair whose units do not convert. One
  primary factor can carry at most one rule of each kind.
- Rules belong to the view. They are edited while the inventory is `DRAFT`,
  refused with 409 in any other state, frozen with the view, copied by
  **Copy the view from** and by a correction (spec 05.3), and serialized in
  the frozen inputs of the run (spec 07.5).
- The inventory page shows the rules in an **Upstream rules** card with the
  two factors, the kind, and how many included records the rule applies to.

### Derived lines

- For every included line whose factor is a rule's primary factor and whose
  scope is `SCOPE_1` or `SCOPE_2`, the run adds a **derived line**: scope 3,
  category `FUEL_ENERGY_RELATED`, the same record, facility, entity,
  country, period, converted quantity, accounting share, and period share as
  the primary line, the upstream factor's CO2e per unit under the run's GWP
  set, and a `derivedFromLineId` that points at the primary line. Its note
  reads "well-to-tank of ACT-0012 Fleet diesel" or "transmission and
  distribution losses of ACT-0031 Grid electricity".
- A derived line carries the primary line's stream, data quality tier,
  uncertainty, and evidence, and is marked as a proxy only when the primary
  line is. It never carries a market-based figure, an instrument, or a lease
  type.
- Transmission-loss lines derive from the **consumed kilowatt-hours** of the
  primary line, whatever instruments cover it. The Scope 3 Standard's category 3
  method computes losses from the electricity consumed and is silent on the
  market-based balance; this spec applies the location-based factor to every
  consumed kilowatt-hour, the methodology states that choice, and the derived
  line's note says so when the primary line carries an instrument.
- A primary line in scope 3 (contractor diesel classified into purchased
  goods and services) produces no derived line. The contractor's fuel is
  already a scope 3 figure and its upstream emissions belong to the
  contractor's own category 3.
- Derived lines appear among the snapshot lines, in the by-category table
  under category 3, in the scope 3 total, in the by-facility, by-entity and
  by-country tables, in the by-gas table, in the lines CSV with a
  `derived_from_line_id` column, and in the factor table (the upstream
  factor is snapshotted like any other). They never carry scope 1 or scope
  2, so nothing is counted twice.

### Gates

- The scope 3 cross-check of spec 07.6 counts the categories a run would
  quantify, including category 3 through the rules, so a declared category
  3 stops warning as soon as a rule matches an included scope 1 or scope 2
  factor.
- The CLASSIFICATION gate warns when category 3 is declared and no rule
  matches any included scope 1 or scope 2 factor: "Fuel- and energy-related
  activities is declared, but no upstream rule matches a scope 1 or scope 2
  factor in this view; add a rule or say why category 3 is not quantified."
- The gate lists the rules as information: "2 upstream rules: Diesel (100%
  mineral diesel) → Well-to-tank diesel; Grid electricity, Ghana 2024 → T&D
  losses, Ghana (derived)". The report's methodology repeats the list.

### The scope select (finding F34)

- The scope select on the activity view is enabled for every factor. Only a
  lease type disables it, because Appendix F derives the scope (spec 04.1).
  The sentence "This factor's scope is inherent." is gone.
- A scope that departs from the default (the stream's, or the factor's when
  the record has no stream) shows the justification field of spec 04.3, and
  the gate errors until at least 10 characters are recorded. This is what
  specs 04.1 and 04.3 already require; this spec records the defect and its
  regression test.

Given Asante Gold Resources with the rules Diesel (100% mineral diesel) →
Well-to-tank diesel and Grid electricity, Ghana 2024 → T&D losses, Ghana:
when the run is launched, then it adds one derived line for each of the 14
diesel lines in scope 1 and each of the 9 electricity lines in scope 2, the
by-category table shows category 3 with 23 lines and a figure, the report's
declaration table reads the category as quantified and the methodology lists
which of its activities the rules cover ("well-to-tank of fuels; transmission
and distribution losses") and which are not quantified ("upstream emissions of
purchased electricity: no factor"), and the CSV row of each
derived line names its parent line. Given the head-office electricity line
covered by an I-REC: the transmission-loss line still derives from its full
6,400 kWh, and its note says "on the consumed kWh, not the market-based
balance". Given the camp landfill record with the "Waste to landfill"
factor: the scope select offers scope 1, the justification field appears,
and the gate is silent once "company-operated landfill on site" is recorded.

## API

- `GET /inventories/{id}/upstream-rules` → `{id, primaryFactorId,
  primaryFactorName, upstreamFactorId, upstreamFactorName, kind,
  matchingLines}[]`.
- `POST /inventories/{id}/upstream-rules` `{primaryFactorId,
  upstreamFactorId, kind}` → 201; 409 `Operation not allowed` when the
  inventory is not `DRAFT`; 422 `errors.upstreamFactorId` when the units do
  not convert or the factor is not approved; 409 when the primary factor
  already carries a rule of that kind; 404 for a factor of another
  organization.
- `DELETE /inventories/{id}/upstream-rules/{ruleId}` → 204; 409 unless
  `DRAFT`.
- `InventoryRequest.copyFromInventoryId` and `POST /inventories/{id}/supersede`
  copy the rules with the view (spec 05.3).
- `RunLineResponse` gains `derivedFromLineId` and `derivedKind` (null on a
  primary line). `ReportResponse.byScope3Category` counts derived lines;
  `ReportResponse.methodology` gains `upstreamRules[]`. The lines CSV gains
  `derived_from_line_id` and `derived_kind` after `line_id`; `inputs.json`
  gains `upstreamRules`.
- `ValidationResponse` (the pre-flight) carries the new warning and the
  information line under `CLASSIFICATION`.

## Non-goals and open questions

- Upstream emissions of purchased electricity (generation fuel supply chain)
  as a third kind; the Ghana pack has no such factor yet. Add the kind when
  a factor exists.
- Rules at organization level that every new inventory starts from; a copied
  view carries them for now.
- A derived line for a record whose primary factor publishes CO2e only
  inherits nothing about gases; the by-gas reconciliation is spec 07.7.
- Should the rule also fire for a primary line in scope 3 category 8
  (upstream leased assets under equity share)? The Standard is silent;
  proposed: no, and the note on the category 8 line says upstream emissions
  of a leased asset are optional under the Technical Guidance for category 8
  and are not quantified. Open for the owner.
