# 02.7: Adopting a new edition

- **Status**: Approved (2026-09-13)
- **Protocol**: Chapter 1 (consistency: one factor vintage across an
  inventory and across years), Chapter 5 (base year, and the method change
  and error triggers for recalculation), Chapter 9 (the methodology and the
  factor vintage are reported), ISO 14064-1:2018 clause 8.2 (records of the
  decisions behind a reported figure)
- **Owner**: Michael Takrama
- **Created**: 2026-09-13 (the emission factor pack maintenance console)
- **Modules**: `ghg` (`FactorPackNotice`, `FactorPackAdoptionService`,
  `web/FactorPackNoticeController`, `BaseYearService`, `GhgAuditEvent`),
  `src/features/ghg` (`FactorPackUpdatesPage`, `AdoptionDiffDrawer`,
  `OrganizationLayout` nav)

## Problem

Spec 02.5 lets a platform maintainer publish a new edition of a factor pack.
Spec 02.6 defines what importing that edition does to an organization's
factors. Between the two sits the question neither answers: who decides that
a client's numbers change, and when.

Today an import overwrites a factor's values in place, so a maintainer who
changed a pack could move a preparer's numbers with nobody in the client
organization told. The consistency principle (ch. 1) assumes one deliberate
factor vintage per inventory, chapter 5 treats a method change as something a
company decides and documents rather than something its software applies, and
ISO 14064-1:2018 clause 8.2 expects that decision to be retrievable.

An edition is the unit of vintage, and adopting one is an accounting
decision. It belongs to the organization, not to the platform.

## Behavior

### Publishing changes nothing

Publishing an edition (spec 02.5) writes to the catalogue and to the notices
of this spec. It never writes to `ghg_emission_factors`, to any assignment,
or to any run. Every organization's figures are the same the moment after a
publication as the moment before.

For each organization holding at least one factor whose lineage appears in
the predecessor edition, publication raises one **notice**. An organization
holding none gets none.

Given Asante Gold Resources holding 56 lineages of `sector-mining`, when the
maintainer publishes `sector-mining-2027`, then one open notice exists for
Asante, every one of its factor values is unchanged, and its last run's total
is unchanged.

### The notice

A notice carries the new edition, the predecessor it supersedes, when it was
raised, how many of the organization's lineages the edition touches, how many
move by more than five percent, the estimated movement in kg CO2e, and a
**diff hash** over the per-row comparison. The hash is what a verifier uses to
confirm the diff the decider saw is the diff the record describes.

The organization reads its notices in its own workspace, under **Emission
factors**, on a page named **Updates**, whose navigation entry carries a count
of open notices. Notices are not an administrator's page.

A notice is `OPEN`, `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED`, or `WITHDRAWN`. Withdrawing an
edition (spec 02.5) closes every open notice for it as `WITHDRAWN`, and the
page says the edition was withdrawn by the publisher.

### The diff

Opening a notice shows, per lineage the edition touches:

- the code, the name and the unit,
- the value the organization holds now and the value the edition carries,
- the absolute and the percent change,
- which gas components changed,
- whether the provenance changed, that is the publication, its year, the
  data year, or the Global Warming Potential basis; a basis differing from
  the version the organization holds is shown as a banner and can never be
  answered as a vintage progression, because chapter 1 requires one basis
  across the inventory and across years,
- the estimated movement in kg CO2e, computed over the organization's
  current draft period using the activity data already recorded.

Three groups are listed apart, because the decision does not apply to them:

- **Conflicts**: lineages the organization has edited locally. Spec 02.6
  never touches these, whatever the decision, and the page says so.
- **Blocked**: lineages whose change would fall inside a period that is
  `FROZEN`, `FINAL`, or `PUBLISHED`.
- **Discontinued**: lineages the organization holds that the new edition
  does not carry. Accepting does not retire them. The page names them and
  says a retirement is a separate decision under spec 02.6.
- **Earlier periods**: inventories whose period ends before `applies_from`,
  which raise coverage warnings once the edition is accepted because the new
  version does not cover them, with a line saying the warning is correct and
  is what a vintage means.

The estimated movement is an estimate and says so. It uses current activity
data, which can change before the next run.

### The decision

Accepting or declining is one act by one named person.

- The decider must hold `REVIEWER` or `OWNER`, the roles that already
  designate a final run and publish (spec 01.2). A `PREPARER` records facts.
  Moving a factor vintage is an accounting decision, so it sits with the
  roles that approve one. A `VERIFIER` reads the diff and cannot decide.
  **Accept** and **Decline** therefore join the approve set of spec 01.4,
  beside **Mark as final** and **Publish**, and are disabled for a preparer
  with the tooltip that set already uses. Running an import directly stays in
  the write set, where spec 01.4 places **Import pack** and spec 02.6 leaves
  it.
- **Accept** runs the versioned import of spec 02.6 from the edition's
  `applies_from`.
- **Decline** changes nothing and closes the notice.
- Either way the record keeps the timestamp, both edition ids, the diff
  hash, the decider's email and role, and an optional note.

While any inventory whose period covers `applies_from` is `FROZEN`, `FINAL`,
or `PUBLISHED`, **Accept** is refused with 409 naming that inventory.
Declining stays available, because declining writes no factor.

Given a preparer opening the notice, then the diff is readable and **Accept**
is disabled with "Needs the Reviewer or Owner role." Given the reviewer
accepting, then the import runs, the notice reads accepted with their name,
and the organization's next run uses the new values.

### The recalculation question

Accepting requires an answer to one question, because chapter 5 treats the
three cases differently:

| Answer | What it means | Chapter 5 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Vintage progression** | The edition applies to the next reporting year forward | Not a recalculation trigger while the estimated movement is below the organization's significance threshold; at or above that threshold it is a methodology change under chapter 5 and is assessed like a retrospective adoption. The methodology note records the vintage change and the percentage it was measured against |
| **Retrospective adoption** | The edition is applied to a year already reported | A method change. Assess against the threshold |
| **Erratum** | The edition corrects a wrong value in a year already reported | An error correction. Assess against the threshold |

The answer is stored on the notice in every case, so a verifier can see the
question was asked and how it was answered. That record is the "not
triggered" evidence. No candidate is raised for a vintage progression whose
estimated movement is below the significance threshold; above it, a
`METHODOLOGY_CHANGE` candidate is raised as for a retrospective adoption.

The report's base-year section prints the answer, so it reaches a reader and
not only the database. Each accepted notice appears there with the edition,
the answer, the affected percent, and the threshold it was measured against,
beside the recalculations spec 06.1 already lists. A verifier reading a
report therefore sees every vintage decision behind it without opening the
product.

For the two answers that warrant one, and for a vintage progression at or
above the threshold, the product raises a candidate through
`BaseYearService.raise` with the existing trigger that matches:
`METHODOLOGY_CHANGE` for a retrospective adoption or an above-threshold
vintage progression, `ERROR_CORRECTION` for an erratum.
`RecalculationTrigger` gains no value, so `V14__base_year.sql`'s check
constraint stands unchanged. The affected share is the estimated
movement as a percentage of the base year's final run total, which is the
`affectedPercent` that `raise` already requires.

Two conditions the accept flow must handle, because `raise` enforces them:

- An organization with no base year cannot carry a candidate. The answer is
  still recorded on the notice, no candidate is raised, and the page says so.
- A candidate above the threshold holds the inventories that report against
  the base year (spec 06.1). The hold is on **Mark as final** and
  **Publish**, never on launching a run: quantifying the movement is how a
  recalculation is assessed and how a restated base year is produced, so a
  preparer must still be able to calculate. The decision screen says so
  before the decider accepts, in these words: "Accepting raises a base-year
  recalculation candidate. If it is above your significance threshold,
  inventories that report against the base year cannot be marked final or
  published until the recalculation is completed or declined."

### Attribution

Accepting and declining are acts on an organization, so they are recorded as
`GhgAuditEvent` with the new actions `FACTOR_PACK_ADOPTED` and
`FACTOR_PACK_DECLINED`, carrying the decider's email and the edition, and
appear at `GET /organizations/{id}/events` beside freezes and publications.

## API

- `GET /organizations/{id}/factor-pack-notices` returns the notices, open
  first, each with `{id, editionId, editionName, predecessorEditionId,
  status, raisedAt, rowsAffected, rowsOverThreshold, estimatedKgCo2eDelta,
  diffHash, decidedAt, decidedBy, decidedByRole, recalculationCase,
  decisionNote}`. Members only, as every organization read is (spec 01.3).
- `GET /factor-pack-notices/{noticeId}/diff` returns `{rows[], conflicts[],
  blocked[], discontinued[], estimatedKgCo2eDelta, diffHash}`. A row carries
  `{code, name, unit, currentKgCo2ePerUnit, newKgCo2ePerUnit, absoluteChange,
  percentChange, gasesChanged[], provenanceChanged, estimatedKgCo2eDelta}`.
- `POST /factor-pack-notices/{noticeId}/accept` `{recalculationCase, note?}`
  returns the import result of spec 02.6. 422 `errors.recalculationCase`
  when the answer is missing or not one of the three. 403 for any role but
  `REVIEWER` and `OWNER`. 409 naming the inventory while a covering period is
  locked, and 409 when the notice is not open.
- `POST /factor-pack-notices/{noticeId}/decline` `{note?}` returns the
  notice. Same role rule, 409 when the notice is not open.
- The report's `baseYear` section (spec 06.1) gains `editionDecisions[]`
  `{editionId, predecessorEditionId, recalculationCase, affectedPercent,
  thresholdPercent, decidedAt, decidedBy, note?}`, one entry per accepted
  notice, so the answer prints beside the recalculations.
- Errors render as RFC 9457 problem details through `GhgFieldException`
  (422), `GhgRuleViolationException` (409), and `RoleRequiredException` (403).

## Data

`V45__factor_pack_notices.sql`. Number assigned in the planned
implementation order; renumber if that order changes.

- `ghg_factor_pack_notices`: `id`, `organization_id` referencing
  `ghg_organizations` and cascading, `edition_id` and
  `predecessor_edition_id` referencing `ghg_factor_pack_editions` (spec
  02.5), `status` checked against the four values, `raised_at`, `diff_hash`,
  `rows_affected`, `rows_over_threshold`, `estimated_kg_co2e_delta`,
  `decided_at`, `decided_by_user_id`, `decided_by`, `decided_by_role`,
  `decision_note`, `recalculation_case` checked against
  `VINTAGE_PROGRESSION`, `RETROSPECTIVE_ADOPTION`, and
  `ERRATUM_ON_REPORTED_YEAR`, `recalculation_id`, and `applied_at`. It also
  keeps `significance_threshold_percent` and `affected_percent` as they
  stood at the decision, and `scopes_affected`, so the record carries what a
  recalculation log needs rather than the answer alone. `decision_note` is
  required whenever the answer is `VINTAGE_PROGRESSION` and the affected
  percent is at or above the threshold, because that is the case a verifier
  questions. Unique on
  `(organization_id, edition_id)`, so a publication raises one notice per
  organization. A check requires a decided notice to carry the decider, the
  time, and the recalculation answer. A partial index on
  `(organization_id) WHERE status = 'OPEN'` serves the count badge.
- `ghg_audit_events`: the action check is dropped and recreated with
  `FACTOR_PACK_ADOPTED` and `FACTOR_PACK_DECLINED` added, the pattern
  `V37__organization_confidentiality_and_deletion.sql` uses. Nothing else on
  the table changes, and `chk_ghg_audit_events_subject` stands.

The migration adds no data and rewrites none.

## Events

None published. `BaseYearService.raise` is called in the same transaction as
an acceptance, so a candidate and the notice commit together or neither does.

## Non-goals and open questions

- Notifying by email when a notice is raised. The inbox and its badge are
  the channel. Spec 01.1 owns outbound mail and no template exists for this.
- Adopting part of an edition. A notice is accepted whole or declined whole,
  because a partial vintage breaks the consistency principle of chapter 1.
  Retiring or hand-editing single lineages afterwards stays available under
  spec 02.6.
- Automatic vintage updates stay a non-goal, as spec 02.1 states.
- Re-opening a declined notice. The organization imports the edition
  directly under spec 02.6 if it changes its mind, which records the import
  without a decision record. Whether a declined notice should be re-openable
  is open.
- Estimating the movement against anything but the current draft period.
- One retrievable recalculation log joining these decisions to the
  candidates of spec 06.1. The report's base-year section prints both, and
  the notice and the candidate each hold their own record, but no single
  endpoint or export returns the pair as one log. Whether a verifier should
  be handed that as one artifact is open.
