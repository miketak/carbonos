# 02.4: Sector pack completeness: gases as themselves, flaring, Montreal Protocol gases, purchased goods

- **Status**: Implemented (2026-09-12)
- **Protocol**: Chapter 4 (the seven Kyoto gas groups; non-Kyoto gases such
  as HCFCs are outside the scopes and may be reported separately), Chapter 6
  (factors appropriate to the source), Chapter 9 (each gas in tonnes and
  tonnes CO2e; optional information reported separately), Scope 3 Standard
  category 1 (purchased goods, minimum boundary)
- **Owner**: Michael Takrama
- **Created**: 2026-09-12 (audit 2026-09-11, findings F56 and the factor part
  of F26)
- **Modules**: `ghg` (`EmissionFactor`, `FactorPacks`, `GhgRunLine`, the run,
  `ReportAssembler`, `ReportPdf`), `src/features/ghg` (emission factors
  page, report page), `scripts/gen-factor-packs.py`

## Problem

The sector packs promise more than they hold. The mining card says "lime and
cement as purchased goods, grinding media and steel", but for a tonne record
the picker offers only the IPCC calcination rows (scope 1) and construction
aggregates and concrete; purchased lime, the largest category 1 line at a
Ghanaian gold plant, had to be forced through the calcination factor as a
proxy. The oil and gas card says methane from venting "uses the site gas
composition with the GWP set, which the refrigerants pack does not carry",
and no pack carries a row for methane, nitrous oxide or carbon dioxide as a
gas, nor any flaring row. HCFC-22, still the common refrigerant in West
African air conditioning, has no route: the refrigerants pack has no
Montreal Protocol row, so the R-22 top-up could only be excluded with a
typed estimate of zero, and its tonnage cannot be disclosed. Worse, the
DEFRA pack does carry twenty Montreal Protocol rows, classified scope 1
fugitive with a CO2e value, so a preparer who imports it can put R-22 into
the scope 1 total, which Chapter 4 forbids. Land clearing and tailings have
no method, and the cards do not say so.

## Behavior

- **Gases as themselves.** The shared library gains one row per gas, per
  kilogram of gas emitted: **Methane (CH4) emitted as gas**, **Nitrous oxide
  (N2O) emitted as gas**, **Carbon dioxide (CO2) emitted as gas**, **Sulfur
  hexafluoride (SF6) emitted as gas** and **Nitrogen trifluoride (NF3) emitted
  as gas**. Each carries a single per-gas component of 1 kg per kg and no
  other, so its CO2e follows the inventory's GWP set through the existing
  per-gas arithmetic: 1 kg of fossil methane is 28 kg CO2e under AR5 and 29.8
  under AR6; nitrous oxide 265 and 273; SF6 23,500 and 25,200; NF3 16,100 and
  17,400 (IPCC AR5 and AR6 100-year potentials as `GwpSet` records them). The
  stated `kgCo2ePerUnit` is the AR5 value. The methane row exists twice,
  fossil and biogenic origin, because AR6 gives them different potentials.
  Default scope 1, category fugitive emissions, scope-agnostic. Source: "IPCC
  AR5 WG1 Table 8.A.1; IPCC AR6 WG1 Table 7.SM.7 (fossil methane Table 7.15),
  100-year GWP; the mass is the activity datum", as `GwpSet` cites them. A
  vented or fugitive mass derived from a gas composition is recorded against
  these rows; the composition arithmetic stays with the preparer (a non-goal
  here).
- **Flaring.** The oil and gas pack gains flaring rows. IPCC 2006 Volume 2
  chapter 4 publishes Tier 1 flaring defaults per unit of oil or gas
  *produced* (Tables 4.2.4 and 4.2.5, in Gg per 10^6 m3 produced), and, in
  footnote (e) of Table 4.2.5, a default per cubic metre *flared* at a stated
  efficiency and gas analysis, which this spec did not know existed when it
  was written. Every row states the conversion from the published Gg per
  volume to the unit it carries. The pack ships four rows, all scope 1
  stationary combustion and all unapproved until the preparer confirms the
  segment and the practice.
  The values were fetched from
  https://www.ipcc-nggip.iges.or.jp/public/2006gl/vol2.html and are cited by
  table, row and unit; none is estimated.
  (a) **Flaring, gas production (per 1000 m3 gas produced)**: Table 4.2.4
  (developed countries), Gas Production, Flaring, IPCC code 1.B.2.b.ii, CH4
  7.6E-07, CO2 1.2E-03 and N2O 2.1E-08 Gg per 10^6 m3 of gas produced. One Gg
  per 10^6 m3 is one kg per m3, so the row is CO2 1.2, CH4 0.00076 and N2O
  0.000021 kg per thousand cubic metres. The row is per **thousand** cubic
  metres, not per cubic metre as this spec first said: the CH4 and N2O
  components per m3 (7.6E-07 and 2.1E-08) fall below the six decimals a
  factor column stores, and a per-m3 row would round the nitrous oxide away.
  The unit registry gains `1000m3` in the volume dimension, so a record in m3
  still converts. Table 4.2.5 (developing countries and economies in
  transition) carries the same figures as the lower bound of its range, up to
  CH4 1.0E-06, CO2 1.6E-03 and N2O 2.9E-08; the row's citation says so.
  (b) **Flaring, oil production (per m3 oil produced)**: Table 4.2.4, Oil
  Production, Conventional Oil, Flaring, IPCC code 1.B.2.a.ii, CH4 2.5E-05,
  CO2 4.1E-02 and N2O 6.4E-07 Gg per 10^3 m3 of conventional oil produced,
  which is CO2 41, CH4 0.025 and N2O 0.00064 kg per m3 produced.
  (c) **Flared gas, per m3 flared (IPCC default composition)**: Table 4.2.5,
  footnote (e), which publishes a direct per-volume-flared factor this spec
  did not know existed: CH4 0.012, CO2 2.0 and N2O 0.000023 Gg per 10^6 m3 of
  gas flared at a flaring efficiency of 98% and a typical gas analysis at a
  gas processing plant (91.9% CH4, 0.58% CO2, 0.68% N2 and 6.84% non-methane
  hydrocarbons by volume), which is the same figures in kg per m3 flared.
  This is the row to use where the flared volume is metered; the 2% that
  escapes uncombusted is already in its CH4 component.
  (d) a proxy row **Flared gas, per m3 flared, as natural gas fully combusted
  (proxy)** built from the DEFRA 2026 "Gaseous fuels: Natural gas, per cubic
  metre" row (2.02633 kg CO2e, of which CO2 2.02231, CH4 0.00010964, N2O
  0.00000358; data year 2026), whose note says it assumes complete combustion
  of a natural-gas composition and that the uncombusted fraction is recorded
  separately against the methane row. Flare efficiency and composition as
  inputs are non-goals; a site with a measured composition enters a
  supplier-specific factor.
- **Reporting basis.** A factor carries a **reporting basis**: `SCOPES` (the
  default) or `OUTSIDE_SCOPES_NON_KYOTO`. A line calculated with an
  `OUTSIDE_SCOPES_NON_KYOTO` factor is a snapshot line like any other, with
  its mass of gas and an informational CO2e, but it is left out of every scope
  total, the by-scope tables, the by-gas table and the intensity figures. The
  report prints it in a block **Gases outside the scopes (Montreal Protocol)**
  after biogenic CO2, numbered **6a** on the page and in the PDF so the
  sections that follow keep their numbers, one row per gas: gas, kilograms of
  gas, a **basis** column (`FACTOR` for a calculated line, `RECORDED_MASS` for a record
  excluded under the reason of spec 04.8, which adds its rows to this block),
  the CO2e the source publishes for information (none for a recorded mass)
  with its GWP set named in the column ("149,600 kg CO2e, AR5 as published by
  DESNZ 2026"), never rescaled, and when the inventory's GWP set differs the
  block adds "AR5 potentials as published; not restated to AR6",
  the factor name or record reference, and the sentence "Reported separately
  as optional information under Chapter 4 and Chapter 9; not included in any
  scope." The PDF and the lines CSV carry the basis on each line. The
  EMISSION_FACTOR gate warns when an `OUTSIDE_SCOPES_NON_KYOTO` line exists so
  the report block is expected, and errors when such a factor is classified
  into scope 2 or scope 3 unless a lease type derived that scope; the scope is
  informational on a line outside the scopes.
- **Montreal Protocol rows.** The DEFRA pack's twenty "Montreal protocol
  products" rows (the "Emissions including only non-Kyoto products" rows; the
  "Total emissions" duplicates are dropped) carry the
  `OUTSIDE_SCOPES_NON_KYOTO` basis, default scope 1 fugitive emissions:
  HCFC-22 (R-22) at 1,760 kg CO2e per kg, CFC-11 4,660, CFC-12 10,200 and the
  rest as DEFRA 2026 publishes them (UK Government conversion factors 2026,
  Refrigerant and other, AR5). The refrigerants pack and both the mining and
  oil and gas sector packs select the HCFC-22 row. The names of these rows are
  the list of **known non-Kyoto gases** that spec 04.8's exclusion dialog
  defaults from. DEFRA's "Montreal protocol products" group holds exactly
  twenty rows, and each is renamed to the gas itself: HCFC-22 (R-22),
  HCFC-21, HCFC-123, HCFC-124, HCFC-141b, HCFC-142b, HCFC-225ca, HCFC-225cb,
  CFC-11 (R-11), CFC-12 (R-12), CFC-13, CFC-113, CFC-114, CFC-115,
  Halon-1211, Halon-1301, Halon-2402, Carbon tetrachloride, Methyl bromide
  and Methyl chloroform. R-401A, R-402A, R-408A and R-409A are
  HCFC-containing blends in the EPA GWP table, not rows of DEFRA's Montreal
  group, so they keep the `SCOPES` basis their source gives them. A migration sets the basis on any
  organization copy of those rows already imported. A record excluded because
  no factor exists for a non-Kyoto gas uses the `OUTSIDE_SCOPES_NON_KYOTO`
  exclusion reason of spec 04.8; that spec owns the exclusion path, this one
  the factor path.
- **Purchased goods in the mining pack.** DEFRA 2026 "Material use" has no
  lime or cement row. The mining pack selects **Construction: Concrete,
  primary material production** (118.80307 kg CO2e per tonne) and
  **Construction: Metals, primary material production** (3,821.94858 kg CO2e
  per tonne) as scope 3 category 1 rows, labels the metals row in its note as
  a construction-metal average usable for steel and grinding media (a factor
  carries no proxy flag of its own: the proxy flag and its justification live
  on the record's classification, spec 04.3, and the note tells the preparer
  to tick it), and ships **supplier-factor templates** for **Quicklime purchased
  (supplier factor)**, **Cement purchased (supplier factor)**, **Sodium
  cyanide purchased (supplier factor)** and **Grinding media purchased
  (supplier factor)**: unapproved organization factors per tonne, scope 3
  category 1, with every value zero and a note that says which document to
  obtain (the supplier's product carbon footprint or environmental product
  declaration) and that the run is blocked until the value is entered and the
  factor approved. The pack card's text is rewritten to say exactly this. The
  IPCC calcination rows stay scope 1, for a site that calcines.
- **Land clearing and tailings.** IPCC 2006 Volume 4 chapter 4 gives default
  above-ground biomass per hectare by ecological zone (Table 4.7) and
  root-to-shoot ratios (Table 4.4), from which a per-hectare CO2 loss for
  clearing follows with the default carbon fraction (Table 4.3). The mining
  pack ships one row, **Land clearing, tropical moist forest (per hectare)**,
  unapproved, fetched and cited from
  https://www.ipcc-nggip.iges.or.jp/public/2006gl/vol4.html: Table 4.7
  above-ground biomass, tropical moist deciduous forest, Africa, 260 tonnes
  d.m. per hectare; Table 4.4 root-to-shoot ratio 0.24 for tropical moist
  deciduous forest above 125 tonnes per hectare; Table 4.3 carbon fraction
  0.47 tonne C per tonne d.m. for tropical and subtropical. 260 x 1.24 x 0.47
  x 44/12 is 555.60 tonnes CO2 per hectare, so the row carries 555,602.666667
  kg CO2 per hectare. The unit registry gains `hectare`, `km2` and `m2` in a
  new area dimension. Until a land-use category exists the row
  defaults to scope 1 process emissions with a note naming the Land Sector and
  Removals Guidance, which amortizes land-use-change CO2 over 20 years, so the
  one-shot per-hectare value is a placeholder until that treatment exists. Tailings and mine-water treatment stay a supplier or
  study factor entered by hand, and the mining card says so.

Given the Asante R-22 top-up of 85 kg classified with HCFC-22: the run line
reads 85 kg of gas, 149,600 kg CO2e for information, and no scope total moves;
the report's outside-the-scopes block lists it, and the by-gas table still
foots to the total. Given a 12,000 kg methane vent at a gas plant
classified with the fossil methane row under AR5: 336,000 kg CO2e in scope 1,
12,000 kg CH4 in the by-gas table, and 357,600 kg CO2e in a copied AR6 view.

## API

- `EmissionFactorResponse`, `CreateEmissionFactorRequest` and the pack row
  gain `reportingBasis` (`SCOPES` default, `OUTSIDE_SCOPES_NON_KYOTO`).
- `RunLineResponse` gains `reportingBasis`; `ReportResponse` gains
  `outsideScopes[]` `{gas, kg, basis, kgCo2eInformational?, factorName?,
  recordRefs[]}` (basis `FACTOR` here; spec 04.8 adds `RECORDED_MASS`
  rows) and its totals exclude those lines. `lines.csv` gains a
  `reporting_basis` column.
- `PUT /assignments/{id}/classify`: 409 when an `OUTSIDE_SCOPES_NON_KYOTO`
  factor is given a scope other than scope 1.
- Pack cards (`GET /factor-packs`) carry the rewritten notes.

## Data

`V39__reporting_basis_and_gas_rows.sql` (Number assigned in the planned
implementation order of the audit backlog; renumber if that order changes.):
`reporting_basis varchar(30) not null default 'SCOPES'` on
`ghg_emission_factors`, `ghg_run_factors` and `ghg_run_lines`; the six gas
rows seeded into the shared library; existing organization copies of the DEFRA
Montreal rows updated to `OUTSIDE_SCOPES_NON_KYOTO`. The unit registry is code,
not a table, so `hectare` (with `km2` and `m2`) and `1000m3` are added to
`UnitConverter` rather than by the migration. Setting the basis rewrites data:
a run launched after it leaves those masses out of the scope totals, while a
run launched before it keeps its figures, because run lines and run factors
are snapshots the migration does not touch. The pack JSON files regenerated: DEFRA Montreal rows with the basis,
the refrigerants and sector packs selecting HCFC-22, the flaring, proxy,
material and template rows, and the rewritten card notes.

## Non-goals and open questions

- Composition and flare-efficiency inputs that derive CO2 and CH4 from a
  volume flared or vented: the preparer computes the masses and records
  them against the gas rows or a supplier-specific factor.
- A land-use-change category in `ActivityCategory`, and removals: open
  until the Land Sector and Removals Guidance is adopted in a spec.
- Methane from tailings and mine water, coal-mine methane defaults (IPCC
  2006 Volume 2 chapter 4, Table 4.1.4): a later pack.
