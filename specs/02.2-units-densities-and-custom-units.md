## Problem

Conversions within a dimension worked and printed on the line (m3 to litre,
MWh to kWh), but nothing crossed the divide between mass and volume. LPG,
heavy fuel oil and coal are invoiced by mass in Ghana and diesel arrives in
drums or tonnes at remote sites, while most fuel factors are per litre. An
LPG record in kilograms could match only factors that happened to be per
tonne; a "drum (200 L)" unit was told it would not convert, no factor
matched, and the record ended up excluded and corrected after publication.

## Behavior

- **Custom units.** An organization defines a unit as a multiple of a
  registered unit: `1 drum = 200 litre`, `1 bag = 50 kg`. The unit picker
  offers it with its definition; records in it convert like any unit of the
  base unit's dimension, and the line prints the definition it applied. A
  code that is already a registered unit is refused; a unit records are
  recorded in cannot be deleted.
- **Densities.** A density is a material's kg per litre with its source. The
  shared library ships **typical values** for common fuels (diesel, petrol,
  LPG, kerosene, heavy fuel oil, biodiesel, lubricating oil) as planning
  values; an organization records its own from the supplier's certificate of
  analysis. A shared value cannot be changed; a density a classification
  applies cannot be deleted.
- **Mass meets volume through a density.** When a record's unit and a
  factor's unit are one mass and one volume, the classification names the
  density that converts between them; the picker offers factors across the
  divide once the organization has any density. The gate errors while no
  density is chosen and warns while a typical value is in use ("replace it
  with the supplier's specification before a final run"). The run converts
  mass to kilograms, divides by the density into litres and on into the
  factor's unit (or the reverse), and the line prints the arithmetic:
  "12 tonne = 12000 kg ÷ 0.8325 kg/litre = 14414.414414 litre (density of
  Diesel (GOIL, 2025 CoA))".
- **Every line says how it converted.** The line carries the density's
  material and figure and a conversion note; the calculation file carries
  them as columns.
- An unregistered unit still matches a factor in the identical unit only,
  and the form says to define it under Units.

Given 12 tonnes of diesel classified with the per-litre diesel factor and
the GOIL density of 0.8325 kg per litre: the line reads 14,414.414 litre and
38,342.342 kg CO2e. Given 5 drums with `1 drum = 200 litre` defined: the
line reads 1,000 litre and 2,660 kg CO2e, with the definition beside it.

## API

- `GET /organizations/{id}/units` → the registry plus the organization's
  custom units, each `{code, label, dimension, toCanonical, custom,
  definition}`. `GET /units` keeps returning the registry.
- `GET|POST /organizations/{id}/custom-units` `{code, label, baseUnit,
  factor}`, `PUT|DELETE /custom-units/{id}`: 422 `errors.code` for a
  registered or duplicate code, `errors.baseUnit` for an unregistered base;
  409 to delete a unit records use.
- `GET|POST /organizations/{id}/densities` `{material, kgPerLitre, source,
  note?}`, `PUT|DELETE /densities/{id}`: 422 `errors.material` for a
  duplicate; 409 to change or delete a shared value, or to delete one a
  classification applies. `DensityResponse.typical` marks a shared value.
- `ClassifyRequest` gains `densityId?`; 422 `errors.densityId` when the
  units need one. `AssignmentResponse` gains `densityId`, `densityMaterial`
  and `densityKgPerLitre`.
- `RunLineResponse` gains `densityMaterial`, `densityKgPerLitre` and
  `conversionNote`; `lines.csv` gains the same columns.

## Non-goals and open questions

- Calorific values (mass or volume to energy); a factor per unit of energy
  is chosen directly, or a factor per mass or volume from the packs.
- Temperature correction of densities; the supplier's figure at its stated
  temperature is what the record cites.
- Published density tables with a citation; the typical values are marked
  as such and the gate warns until a supplier's figure replaces them.
