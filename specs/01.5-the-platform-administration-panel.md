# 01.5: The platform administration panel

- **Status**: Approved
- **Protocol**: Corporate Standard chapter 7 (inventory quality management:
  controlled access and change control), chapter 9 (a published report is a
  record the company issued), ISO 14064-1:2018 clause 8.2 (retention of the
  records that support the inventory), ISO 14064-3 (what a verifier tests of
  the information system and its controls)
- **Owner**: Michael Takrama
- **Created**: 2026-09-14
- **Modules**: `platform` (new: `PlatformSettings`, the settings table and
  its change log), `user` (the accounts summary), `ghg` (`GhgAccess`,
  `SupportAccessService`, `SupportAccess`, `GhgService`,
  `FactorPackAdoptionService`, the platform summary),
  `src/features/admin` (the shell, the dashboard, access requests,
  settings), `src/components` (the shared application header)

## Problem

CarbonOS has four administration pages and no administration area. There is
no route at `/admin`: it matches nothing and renders an empty page. Each
page carries its own copy of a sticky bar whose hardcoded list links to the
pages the caller is not on, so the organizations list is reachable only from
inside another administration page. A platform administrator signs in and
lands on the tenant welcome card with two link buttons added, which does not
link to the organizations list at all.

Nothing tells an administrator what is waiting. Pending access requests, the
one queue with a service-level expectation, sit in a section at the top of
the users list. Draft factor pack editions, active support grants and their
expiry are visible only by opening each page in turn. Every one of those
numbers is already derivable from endpoints that exist.

Two platform decisions are constants in Java. The support-access window is
`Duration.ofHours(24)` in an entity, and any signed-in user may create a
reporting organization. A hosted deployment needs both to be the operator's
choice, and a verifier needs the record of who changed them.

## Behavior

### The administration area

- A platform administrator has an area at `/admin` with its own shell: a
  sidebar of **Dashboard**, **Access requests**, **Users**,
  **Organizations**, **Factor packs** and **Platform settings**, and a link
  back to the rest of the product. **Access requests** carries a count of
  the requests still pending.
- Signing in as an administrator lands on the dashboard. A member who opens
  any `/admin` address gets the access-denied page and never sees the sidebar.
  (Signing in as a member was unchanged here; spec 01.6 changed it, and
  replaced the link back to the rest of the product with a constant
  **GHG accounting** entry.)
- Every administration address that worked before this spec still works.

### The dashboard

- **Needs your attention** lists only what has an action, each row linking
  to the page that clears it: access requests still pending, draft factor
  pack editions (saying how many the reader may approve, because an approver
  may not be the curator), support grants the reader holds with their
  expiry, and a warning when the reader is the only active administrator.
  A row is absent when its count is zero. When every count is zero the
  section says nothing is waiting.
- A draft edition row links to the edition, never to a one-click approval.
  Spec 02.5 requires the approver to check the draft against the source
  document, so the approval stays where the evidence and its checksum are.
- **The platform** counts live organizations, users by state, pack families
  and editions by state, and open adoption notices as a single number.
- **Recent activity** lists platform acts newest first: editions published,
  superseded, withdrawn and evidenced, and support access assumed, ended and
  expired, including grants that closed in the last 30 days.
- **The panel carries no tenant inventory data.** It may name an
  organization and count it. It must not carry a facility count, an
  inventory, a run, a figure in CO2e, or any per-organization breakdown of
  adoption notices. An administrator is an outsider to an organization until
  they assume logged support access (spec 01.3), and a notice states a
  movement computed from the tenant's own activity data. The per-tenant view
  of a publication's effect stays in the blast radius of spec 02.5, where an
  operator decision justifies it.

### Platform settings

- An administrator sets **how long support access lasts**, between 1 and 72
  hours, 24 by default. Seven days of owner-equivalent access to a client's
  pre-publication inventory is standing access with an expiry attached, not
  break-glass. Ending a grant and assuming it again is cheap, and it writes
  a fresh reason into the organization's history, so a short window
  documents a long support case better than a long window does.
- An administrator sets **who may create a reporting organization**:
  everyone signed in, or administrators only.
- Changing either needs a reason of at least 10 characters. The change is
  recorded with the previous value, the new value, who made it, when, and
  the reason, and the record is append-only and readable in the panel.
  Without it an administrator could raise the window, assume access, and
  lower it again, leaving no evidence of a self-serving change to a
  privileged-access control.
- The window in force is readable by any signed-in user, because it is a
  control a client is entitled to know.

### What the window does and does not move

- A grant records the moment it expires when it is taken. Changing the
  setting never moves a grant already in force, in either direction.
- The organization's own history states the window that applied to it.
  "Support access assumed" carries the reason the administrator typed, and
  nothing else: owners read that field as the reason. The window is on the
  grant, which the overview shows with the moment it was taken and the
  moment it expires, and "support access expired" names the duration that
  actually elapsed rather than a constant. Once the window varies, a line
  saying "expired after 24 hours" about a 2-hour grant would be a false
  statement in the record spec 01.3 exists to keep honest.

### Creating an organization

- While creation is open, any signed-in user creates an organization and
  becomes its owner, as before.
- While creation is restricted, only an administrator creates one, and the
  request names the **owner's email**, which must belong to an existing
  account. That user becomes the owner. The administrator is not made a
  member.
- The administrator therefore gets 404 for the organization afterwards, like
  any other outsider, and enters it only by assuming logged support access.
  Seating the administrator instead would give them standing membership of
  every organization on the deployment, with no reason, no expiry and no
  record, holding the deletion and membership rights that support access
  withholds. That is the access spec 01.3 exists to abolish.
- The screen hides **New organization** from a user who may not use it, and
  the server refuses the write whether or not the screen does.

### Support access and the adoption decision

- While support access is active, an administrator may not accept or decline
  a factor pack notice. The refusal is a 403 naming the reason.
- Spec 02.7 reserves that decision to the organization because moving a
  factor vintage changes the organization's numbers. The platform curates
  and publishes the edition, so the platform accepting it on the tenant's
  behalf would put both sides of that decision in one pair of hands.
- **Marking a run final and publishing stay available** under support
  access, so an administrator can still finish a run for a client who cannot.
  Every such act already carries the "under support access" marker in the
  history, and this spec records the difference deliberately: an
  administrator may repair, but not choose the tenant's methodology.

Given an administrator with no memberships: their dashboard shows three
access requests, one draft edition they may not approve because they curated
it, and no support grants; they set the window to 2 hours with the reason
"tightening after the Q3 review"; a grant taken the day before still expires
24 hours after it was taken, and the next grant expires in 2 hours; they
restrict creation, create "Sankofa Gold plc" naming an existing account as
its owner, and then get 404 opening it.

## API

- `GET /api/admin/summary/accounts` (`user`) returns
  `{usersTotal, usersActive, usersPending, administrators,
  accessRequestsPending}`.
- `GET /api/admin/summary/platform` (`ghg`) returns
  `{organizations, draftEditions[], activeGrants[], openNotices,
  recentEvents[]}`. A draft edition carries its curator and whether the
  caller may approve it. No inventory data appears in either.
- `GET /api/admin/settings` returns
  `{supportAccessWindowHours, organizationCreation, updatedAt, updatedBy}`.
- `PUT /api/admin/settings` takes
  `{supportAccessWindowHours, organizationCreation, reason}`. A window
  outside 1 to 72, or a reason under 10 characters, is 422 with the field
  named.
- `GET /api/admin/settings/history` returns the change log, newest first.
- `GET /api/platform/settings` returns `{supportAccessWindowHours}` to any
  signed-in user.
- `GET /api/ghg/organizations/capabilities` returns
  `{mayCreateOrganization}` to any signed-in user.
- `POST /api/ghg/organizations` takes an `ownerEmail`, which only an
  administrator may use: 403 for a non-administrator while creation is
  restricted, and 404 for an email with no account. A non-administrator's
  `ownerEmail` is ignored, because an organization they cannot open is not
  one they meant to create.
- `POST /api/ghg/factor-pack-notices/{id}/accept` and `/decline` are 403
  under support access, naming the reason.
- Everything under `/api/admin/**` is 403 for a member and 401 for an
  anonymous caller, as before.

## Data

`V48__platform_settings.sql` creates two tables.

- `platform_settings`: one row, forever (`CHECK (id = 1)`), with
  `support_access_window_hours` (`CHECK` between 1 and 72, default 24),
  `organization_creation` (`CHECK` in `EVERYONE`, `ADMINISTRATORS`, default
  `EVERYONE`), `updated_at` and `updated_by`. Typed columns rather than a
  key/value bag, so Hibernate validates the schema and the database refuses
  a bad window as well as the service. The defaults are today's behavior, so
  upgrading changes nothing until somebody opens the page.
- `platform_setting_changes`: append-only, with the setting key, the old and
  new values, the actor's id and email, the reason, and when. A platform act
  has neither an organization nor an inventory, so it cannot live in
  `ghg_audit_events`, whose `chk_ghg_audit_events_subject` requires one; and
  it is not about an edition, so it does not belong in
  `ghg_factor_pack_events` either. It gets its own table for the same reason
  that one does.

`SupportAccess` stops computing its own expiry from a constant and stores
the expiry it was given.

## Events

None. The dashboard reads numbers that are true at the moment it is opened,
which is a synchronous read, not a fact to propagate. The two summary
endpoints stay one per module so that neither module has to learn about the
other: `ghg` already depends on `user`, so a single combined endpoint hosted
in `user` would be a cycle, and a `platform` module that both held the
settings and counted organizations would be a cycle the other way.

## Verification

Automated: `PlatformSettingsApiIntegrationTests` (defaults, an update with
its recorded change, the window and reason refusals, member and anonymous
refusals); `SupportAccessWindowTests` (a grant honours the window in force,
a later change does not move it, and the expiry line states the duration
that elapsed); `GhgApiIntegrationTests` extended (restricted creation seats
the named owner and not the administrator, who then gets 404; capabilities
reports the rule); the adoption tests extended (accept and decline refused
under support access); a summary test per module asserting the shape and
asserting that no inventory figure and no per-organization notice data
appear in the payload; `ModularityTests` with a case pinning `platform` as a
leaf. Frontend tests for the shell, the badge, the dashboard bands, the
settings form and the access-requests record.

Manual: QA procedure 11.

## Non-goals and open questions

- A per-organization support-access window. One value applies to the whole
  deployment, so a support case for one client changes the documented
  control for the others. The record on each grant, in each organization's
  own history, is what a client relies on.
- A filtered view or export of every support grant on one organization for a
  reporting period. The events are in the organization's history and each
  act under a grant is marked, but assembling the period's statement means
  reading it by eye. This is the artifact a client's IT-controls enquiry
  asks for first, and it is recorded in `todo.md` as the follow-up.
- A second platform role separating a pack maintainer from a user
  administrator. One `ADMIN` role stays. Publishing an edition already needs
  two people, because an approver may not be the curator (spec 02.5).
- Per-organization platform settings, and a settings page for anything else.
- Whether **Mark as final** and **Publish** should also be refused under
  support access. This spec says they are not, so that an administrator can
  rescue a client who cannot publish. Revisit if a client objects to the
  operator being able to sign a run at all.
