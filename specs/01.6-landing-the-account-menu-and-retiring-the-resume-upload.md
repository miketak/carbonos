# 01.6: Landing, the account menu, and retiring the resume upload

- **Status**: Approved
- **Protocol**: Corporate Standard chapter 7 (controlled access and change
  control: who is inside an organization, and whether the screen says so),
  ISO 14064-1:2018 clause 8.2 (retention of the records that support the
  inventory, and what is therefore not a record)
- **Owner**: Michael Takrama
- **Created**: 2026-09-14
- **Supersedes**: spec 01.5, "Signing in as a member is unchanged"; spec 01.3,
  "their GHG home lists the organizations they are a member of or hold support
  access to" as the only way into an organization
- **Modules**: `src/features/home` (the landing resolver), `src/components`
  (the shared header and the account menu), `src/features/admin` (the shell's
  way out), `src/features/ghg` (the landing path, the support-access banner,
  the empty state), `src/features/profile` and `user` (the resume retirement),
  `scripts/` (the object purge)

## Problem

Spec 01.5 sent an administrator to `/admin` on sign-in and left the rest of
the landing model alone. Everybody else still lands on `/app`: a welcome card
whose whole content is three link buttons. It is also the only link to
`/app/profile` anywhere in the product, so once a reader is inside the GHG
workspace or the administration panel there is no way to their own profile,
and no way back to the panel. The top bar exists three times: the shared
`AppHeader`, and a hand-rolled copy in each of those two pages.

A member with one organization therefore signs in and clicks twice to reach
the only place they work.

Separately, the profile carries a resume upload. It arrived with the avatar in
one spike, nothing in the product reads it, and no part of an inventory
depends on it.

## Behavior

### Signing in lands where the work is

- `/app` resolves rather than renders. An administrator lands on the platform
  dashboard. Everybody else lands on their organization when they have exactly
  one, and on the GHG accounting list when they have none or several.
- One rule, one place: the sign-in form, the set-password flow and a bookmark
  of `/app` all resolve the same way. A deep link still wins over it, so
  somebody sent to the sign-in page from a page they asked for returns there.
- The single-organization target is the organization **overview**, not any
  other page under it. The overview is where `SupportAccessCard` states who
  from the platform team is inside the organization and until when (spec
  01.3). A member who no longer passes through the list no longer sees its
  **Support access** badge, so the page they land on has to carry the same
  fact. A later change to the index route keeps that true or moves the card.
- Until the session has resolved, the resolver fetches nothing. An
  administrator must not pass through a state that looks like a member and
  pull the membership list on the way, because that list carries a facility
  count which spec 01.5 keeps out of the panel.
- A failed membership lookup lands on the list, which renders the failure.
  A lookup that did not answer must never stand between a reader and the
  product.
- The welcome card is deleted.

### The account menu

- The shared header carries an account menu in the top right: the avatar or an
  initial, the display name, and a menu holding the email, **Edit profile**,
  **Administration** for a platform administrator, and **Sign out**. Because
  `AppHeader` serves the administration shell, the organization workspace and
  the organizations list, it is on every signed-in page.
- **Administration** is hidden while the reader is already in the panel, where
  the sidebar is the navigation. Everywhere else it is the only route back,
  and it grants nothing: the outsider boundary is the server's refusal and the
  support grant (spec 01.3), never the absence of a link.
- The menu carries the session's own name and email and nothing scoped to an
  organization, so it says nothing about a tenant.
- Escape and a click outside close it, focus returns to the trigger, and
  following a link closes it.
- The profile page drops its own header for the shared one.

### The way out of the administration panel

- The panel's sidebar ends with **GHG accounting**, which leaves the panel for
  `/app/ghg`. It is always there.
- It is deliberately **not** conditional on the administrator having an
  organization. Three reasons, and the first is the binding one:
  1. The only list of an administrator's organizations is
     `GET /api/ghg/organizations`, whose payload carries `facilityCount`.
     Spec 01.5 says the panel must not carry a facility count. Reading that
     list to decide whether to draw a link would put tenant inventory data
     into the shell of every administration page.
  2. While organization creation is restricted to administrators (spec 01.5),
     **New organization** lives on `/app/ghg`. Hiding the link from an
     administrator with no membership hides the only screen that creates the
     first organization on a deployment.
  3. The header wordmark points at `/app`, which resolves back to `/admin` for
     an administrator. Without the sidebar entry there is no way out at all.
- There is a fourth reason to prefer a constant entry. That list includes
  organizations held under a live support grant, so a conditional entry would
  appear when a grant opened and vanish when it expired: a nav item behaving
  as an unlabelled state indicator, reading as "I have a GHG area" rather than
  "I am inside a client's inventory on a clock". Grant state stays where specs
  01.3 and 01.5 already put it and label it.

### An administrator under support access is told so on every page

- While `myRole` is `ADMIN`, every page of the organization carries a banner
  naming the organization and the moment the grant expires, and saying that
  every act is recorded in the organization's history. Spec 01.4 gives a
  verifier the same treatment for the same reason.
- Before this spec the only statement of a live grant was `SupportAccessCard`
  on the overview, which was enough while the only way in was the
  organizations list and its badge. A one-click route from the panel means a
  session can be spent in the activity register, so standing and expiry travel
  with it.
- When a grant expires mid-session the organization answers 404, and for a
  platform administrator that page says so as an expiry: support access ends
  on its own, and an administrator holds no standing access without a grant.
  The generic "it may have been deleted" would be a false statement about a
  client's organization.

### The empty organizations list is a landing screen now

- A member with no organizations lands on the list. Its empty state told every
  reader to create one, while **New organization** is correctly hidden from a
  reader who may not (spec 01.5). That is the invisible refusal spec 01.4
  exists to stop, and it is now the first screen such a member sees. The copy
  follows the capability: when creation is restricted it says they are not a
  member of any organization yet and names who to ask.

### The resume upload is retired

- The profile offers a display name and a profile picture. The resume upload,
  its download, and the stored objects are gone.
- A resume supports no part of an inventory and appears in no report, so it is
  not a record ISO 14064-1 clause 8.2 or Corporate Standard chapter 7 asks
  anybody to retain. Deleting it is data minimisation.
- **This hard delete is a deliberate exception**, not a precedent. Specs 01.3,
  04.4 and 05.2 keep tombstones rather than erasing: a correction supersedes,
  a void is recorded, an organization's history survives its acts. Those are
  inventory records. A resume is personal data that never entered the
  inventory, and the exception rests on exactly that, so it extends to nothing
  else in that bucket.

Given Kofi, a preparer of one organization: he signs in and reads its
overview, with any live support grant stated there. Given Abena, a member of
three: she signs in on the list. Given a platform administrator on a fresh
deployment with organization creation restricted and no memberships: they
land on the dashboard, follow **GHG accounting**, create "Sankofa Gold plc"
naming the client's email as owner, and get 404 opening it afterwards. Given
that administrator assuming support access and opening the activity register:
every page says they are inside under support access and when it ends.

## API

`GET /api/profile` no longer returns `hasResume` or `resumeFilename`.
`PUT /api/profile/resume` and `GET /api/profile/resume` are gone, so both are
404. Nothing else changes: the landing rule, the menu and the banner are
display concerns over what the server already returns.

## Data

`V49__drop_user_resume.sql` drops `resume_key`, `resume_content_type` and
`resume_filename` from `users`. `V3__user_media.sql` added them next to the
avatar columns and is never edited; the avatar columns stay.

The stored objects are deleted separately, by `make purge-resumes ENV=<env>`
(`scripts/purge-resumes.sh`), because Flyway cannot reach the object store.
The keys are deterministic, `users/<uuid>/resume`; the script pins the prefix
to `users/` and the pattern to `*/resume`, so it can reach neither
`users/<uuid>/avatar` nor anything under `ghg/`. It refuses to run at all if
the bucket or endpoint resolves empty, because a truncated variable would turn
it into a recursive delete over the bucket that holds the evidence behind
published inventories. It dry-runs by default, needs `--yes` to act, and takes
a typed confirmation for production. It is run after the release is deployed
in an environment, so no live session asks for a key that has gone, and the
dated listing is kept with the release record: who may bulk delete from the
evidence store, and under what control, is a question ISO 14064-3 asks of the
information system. Before production the listing is read for a key belonging
to a real person rather than a test account, and the file is offered back
before it is deleted.

The script is a retirement tool, not standing infrastructure; it can be
deleted once every environment is purged.

## Non-goals and open questions

- A member dashboard. The organization overview is the landing, not a new
  page that would need its own figures and its own spec.
- Landing a member of several organizations on their most recent one. It would
  need a per-user record of where they were last, and the list is one click.
- A user menu that switches organizations. The workspace header already has
  the switcher, where the organization is the context.
- Purging the orphaned objects of any other retired feature. This spec's
  exception is about personal data that is not an inventory record, and covers
  the resume alone.
