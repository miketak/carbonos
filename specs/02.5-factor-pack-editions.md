# 02.5: Factor pack editions: authoring, validation and publication

- **Status**: Approved (2026-09-13)
- **Protocol**: Chapter 6 (choosing emission factors), Chapter 9 (the
  methodology and the source of each factor are disclosed), Chapter 1
  (consistency), ISO 14064-1:2018 clause 9.3.1 (factors reported with their
  origin) and clause 8.2 (records retained)
- **Owner**: Michael Takrama
- **Created**: 2026-09-13 (the request for a maintenance console that
  creates, updates, and deletes emission factor packs)
- **Modules**: `ghg` (`FactorPacks`, `FactorPackEdition`, `FactorPackRow`,
  `FactorPackChange`, `FactorPackEvent`, `FactorPackAdminService`,
  `FactorPackValidation`, `FactorPackPublication`, `FactorPackBlastRadius`,
  `web/FactorPackAdminController`), `src/features/admin`

## Problem

A factor pack is the published methodology behind every number a client
reports. Chapter 9 requires the source of each factor to be disclosed, and
ISO 14064-1:2018 clause 9.3.1 requires the factors used to be reported with
their origin. A verifier re-performing a sample has to say which published
table a figure came from, in which edition, and who put it there.

Today a pack is a JSON file compiled into the server. `FactorPacks` reads
`classpath:factor-packs/*.json` once at startup into an immutable map, so
changing a pack needs a release. The ten files hold 2,836 rows across 2,634
distinct codes. They are output of `scripts/gen-factor-packs.py`, which reads
tables from a directory outside the repository, so nobody else can rebuild a
pack. Nothing records who curated a pack, who checked it against the source
document, or when.

Three gaps follow. A maintainer cannot correct a wrong row without an
engineer. There is no edition, so nothing says a client's figures rest on the
2026 tables rather than the 2027 ones. And because the same identifier can be
rebuilt with different values, a citation to `defra-2026` does not name one
thing.

## Behavior

### Families and editions

A **pack family** is a lineage with a stable key such as `defra`, a name, a
kind (`SOURCE` for a published table, `SECTOR` for a selection from others),
and a summary.

An **edition** is one dated release of that family, for example `defra-2026`
or `defra-2026.r2`. It carries the publication it represents: source, source
URL, publication year, Global Warming Potential (GWP) basis, license,
retrieval date, notes, and the date it applies from. The edition, not the
family, is the unit of vintage, so a citation names one thing forever.

### The lifecycle

| Status | The edition is | Rows are |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `DRAFT` | invisible to organizations | mutable |
| `PUBLISHED` | importable | frozen |
| `SUPERSEDED` | readable, not importable | frozen |
| `WITHDRAWN` | readable, not importable | frozen |

Only a draft is mutable. Once published, an edition's rows, metadata, and
values must never change.

- **Create a draft**: a named source publication, its URL, the publication
  year, and the GWP basis. It starts empty or clones a predecessor's rows.
- **Draft to published**: every row passes validation, an evidence file is
  attached with its SHA-256 checksum, the source document is named, the
  applies-from date is set, and an approver who is not the curator confirms.
  Publication computes and freezes the change log against the predecessor.
- **Published to superseded**: a successor in the family is published.
- **Published to withdrawn**: a reason of at least 10 characters. The edition
  leaves the import list. Rows organizations hold stay as they are.

An **erratum** corrects a transcription error. It is a new edition of the
family, marked as an erratum, and publishing it marks the predecessor
`SUPERSEDED` and records on it the note that it contains an error. The wrong
values are never edited, because reports already rest on them.

Given `defra-2026` published and a wrong row found: when the curator clones
it to `defra-2026.r2`, corrects the row, and an approver publishes it as an
erratum, then `defra-2026` reads `SUPERSEDED` with its error note,
`defra-2026.r2` is importable, and the change log names the one row that
moved.

### Authoring

A platform administrator can create a family, create a draft empty or cloned,
edit a draft's metadata, and add, edit, or delete its rows. A draft that was
never published can be deleted outright. A published edition and its rows can
never be deleted, because clause 8.2 requires the records behind a reported
figure to be retained.

Authoring shipped first, on its own: `FactorPackAdminService`,
`FactorPackValidation`, `web/FactorPackAdminController` and the two console
pages. Publication, the evidence upload, the separation-of-duties gate, the
frozen change log, the blast radius and the notices arrived together in the
phase after it, because each of them is part of one record and shipping half
of it would publish an edition a verifier could not sample.

### Separation of duties

The **curator** builds the draft. The **approver** checks it against the
source document and publishes it. They must be two different people. The
edition records both with the email each held at the time.

Given a curator who tries to publish their own draft: the request returns 422
naming the approver, and the edition stays a draft.

### Validation

Publication is refused while any row breaks a rule. Each is a hard failure,
never a warning, because a published edition is a citation.

1. The **code** has two or more colon-separated segments naming the
   publication and the row. Two are allowed because `GHANA:td-losses` is a
   derivation rather than a row in a table. A code must never change once a
   published edition carries it, because spec 02.6 makes it the lineage key
   of rows organizations already hold.
2. **Provenance** is complete: source publication, a source URL that parses
   as absolute, the publication year, and the data year. The citation an
   import builds from them fits `ghg_emission_factors.source`, which `V45`
   widened to `varchar(2000)`, so a published edition never depends on the
   truncation spec 02.6 keeps as a backstop. An unapproved row stating no value is
   exempt from the four fields: it is a template naming the document to
   obtain, which rule 8 governs, and there is no published URL or year until
   the supplier's document arrives. Four rows of `sector-mining` are exactly
   that.
3. The **unit** is one the registry knows, checked with
   `UnitConverter.dimensionOf`. Free text is refused.
4. The **gas split reconciles**: where the row states any component, the sum
   of each multiplied by its potential under the edition's GWP basis equals
   the stated CO2e per unit within one percent. A row publishing no split
   declares itself CO2e only and is exempt. So is a row whose HFC or PFC mass
   has no composition the basis can convert: a blend without one keeps the
   CO2e its source applied, which is how the whole library treats it, and 211
   of the 2,836 seeded rows are exactly that (the DESNZ refrigerant table
   states one kilogram of gas per kilogram with the publisher's own CO2e).
   Every seeded row that can be reconciled reconciles inside one percent.
5. **Biogenic CO2 is excluded** from the stated CO2e per unit. It is caught
   by rule 4's arithmetic: a row whose split reconciles only once the
   biogenic CO2 is added has folded it into the total, and is refused with
   the figure it should have stated.
6. A **non-Kyoto gas** carries the basis `OUTSIDE_SCOPES_NON_KYOTO` (spec
   02.4). A Montreal Protocol row marked `SCOPES` is refused.
7. The **scope and category agree**, or the row is scope-agnostic.
8. **No value without a source**: a non-zero CO2e with no source publication
   is refused. The only permitted zero is an unapproved template whose note
   names the document to obtain.
9. **Approval is attributable**: a row approved in a published edition
   carries the approver and the moment of publication. This is the one rule
   of the edition rather than the row, and it has nothing to say about a
   draft: publication is what makes an approval attributable. The ten
   editions `V43` seeded are exempt, as `chk_ghg_factor_pack_editions_published`
   exempts them, while `provenance_review` is `SEED_UNCHECKED`. That is now
   the whole of what `SEED_UNCHECKED` carries.

Rules 3 and 7 also exist for an organization's own factors, in
`GhgService.requireFactorFacts`. They are two code paths over two tables, and
neither covers the other: a pack row is validated on publication, an
organization factor on save. A change to one must be made in both.

Three rows used to break rule 2's length clause:
`IPCC:2006:land-clearing-tropical-moist-forest` in `sector-mining`, and
`IPCC:2006:flaring-gas-production` and `IPCC:2006:flaring-per-m3-flared` in
`sector-oil-and-gas`, whose citations run to 528, 572 and 528 characters. They
are hand-written derivations whose detail is the arithmetic rather than a table
row, and they shipped before the catalogue existed. The authoring phase exempted
them through `SEED_UNCHECKED`, which put a hole in the rule: an exemption that
covers a real row is a rule that does not hold.

The publication phase fixed the cause instead. `V45` widens
`ghg_emission_factors.source` to `varchar(2000)`, and `ghg_run_factors.source`
with it because a run snapshots the citation, and the exemption is gone from the
rule. The width comes from what the catalogue can produce rather than from what
it holds today: a citation is the source publication and the publisher's three
taxonomy parts joined by ` / `, so at the widths of `ghg_factor_pack_rows` it is
at most 500 + 2 + 120 + 3 + 200 + 3 + 500 = 1,328 characters, and no row of any
edition can outgrow the column. The 2,836 seeded rows have a median citation of
148 characters and a 99th percentile of 228, so an ordinary row is nowhere near
either figure. `SeededFactorPackEditionsTest` asserts that no row relies on an
exemption at all, and names the three with their lengths so a change to their
detail is noticed.

### The source taxonomy

Each row records `sourceCategory`, `sourceActivity`, and `sourceDetail`
separately rather than concatenated, because concatenation loses what tells
two rows apart. The DESNZ 2026 tables carry 31 categories, 523 activities,
and 74 details. Of the 1,868 rows in `defra-2026`, 1,157 share a display name
with another row. "Gaseous fuels: Butane" appears three times, at 3,033.38,
1.745, and 0.222 kg CO2e per unit, differing only by unit, so picking by name
alone can be wrong by a factor of a thousand.

`FactorPacks.PackFactor` carries only `sourceDetail` today, holding the
concatenation `Fuels / Gaseous fuels / Butane`. The record gains
`sourceCategory` and `sourceActivity` beside it, `ghg_factor_pack_rows`
carries all three, and the seed of `V43` splits each concatenated value back
into its parts. Spec 02.6 carries the three onto every version an import
creates.

The split is publisher-aware, because the concatenation is ambiguous: a DESNZ
activity almost always carries a ' / ' of its own ("Gaseous fuels / Butane"),
while an EPA Hub or Ember detail does not. The rule is in
`scripts/packs_to_seed_sql.py`: one segment is a detail alone, two are a
category and an activity, and from three on a `DEFRA:` code takes the second
and third as the activity while any other code takes the second as the
activity and the rest as the detail. Checked against the source CSV tables
the packs were built from, it reproduces the publisher's own three values for
all 2,722 rows drawn from a published table; the other 114 are hand-written
derivations and templates citing a document rather than a table row, and
their citation becomes the detail. The three rejoin to exactly the string the
JSON held, which is what the citation an import writes quotes, so no
organization's factor changes its source.

The 31 categories, 523 activities and 74 details above are the whole DESNZ
2026 table of 2,678 rows. The pack carries 1,868 of them, so the seeded
`defra-2026` edition holds 26 categories, 516 activities and 67 details.

The publisher's category is not the accounting category. `defaultCategory`
stays the `ActivityCategory` a classification defaults to, and the two must
never be conflated.

### Blast radius

Before publishing or withdrawing, the console shows what the state change
would do, for publication the effect if every holder adopted it, and for
withdrawal the organizations whose open notices would close and the holders
already carrying the edition. Publication itself changes no organization's
data; spec 02.7 governs adoption. The report lists the organizations holding
each affected lineage, their open draft inventories, each row's current and
proposed value with the absolute and percent change, the rows moving more
than 5 percent, the estimated tonnes of CO2e each organization would move
computed from its last completed run, rows an organization edited locally,
rows feeding a locked period, rows that are not approved, and lineages the
predecessor held that this edition drops.

It keys on the row's code, never on a pack tag, because 150 codes appear in
more than one pack file and a holder might carry a row under a sector pack's
tag. The tonnage is an estimate computed from the organization's last completed
run, scaling each line's emissions by how far its factor would move, and the
activity data behind it can change before the next run. Each organization's
comparison is hashed, and that hash is the `diff_hash` its notice carries.

### The ten shipped packs

Migration `V43` seeds the ten shipped packs as published editions with the
row counts they carry today, from 1,868 for `defra-2026` down to 1 for
`nga-2024-explosives`. Each applies from 1 January of its publication year,
and its evidence checksum is the SHA-256 of the JSON file it came from. The
curator reads as the seed and the approver is null, which the
published-edition check allows only while `provenance_review` is
`SEED_UNCHECKED`; the published moment is a fixed timestamp rather than
`now()`, so a wipe rebuilds an identical catalogue; each seeded edition names its source publication and URL,
records that its checksum is over the shipped JSON rather than the
publication, and states that the separation-of-duties record starts at the
next edition. A verifier can therefore see that the ten seeded editions were
not independently checked against their publications, and sample
accordingly. Nothing changes for an organization that already imported one.

## API

Everything sits under `/api/admin/factor-packs`, which `SecurityConfig`
reserves for the `ADMIN` platform role, and `FactorPackAdminService` calls
`GhgAccess.checkAdmin` again on entry, as `SupportAccessService` does. Any
other caller gets 403 titled "Access denied".

- `GET /api/admin/factor-packs` lists families with their editions:
  `{packKey, name, kind, editions[{editionId, status, publicationYear,
  appliesFrom, rowCount, holderCount, curator, approver, publishedAt}]}`.
- `POST /api/admin/factor-packs` creates a family.
- `POST /api/admin/factor-packs/{packKey}/editions` creates a draft, with
  `cloneFrom` naming a predecessor whose rows are copied.
- `GET|PUT|DELETE /api/admin/factor-packs/editions/{editionId}` reads, edits,
  and deletes a draft. Against a published edition, `PUT` and `DELETE` return
  409.
- `GET .../editions/{editionId}/rows` pages the rows, filtered by
  `sourceCategory`, `sourceActivity`, `unit`, and a search term.
  `POST|PUT|DELETE .../rows/{rowId}` author them, each 409 once published.
- `GET .../editions/{editionId}/validation` returns every broken rule as
  `{rule, code, message}`.
- `POST .../editions/{editionId}/evidence` uploads the source document
  through `MediaStorage` and returns its key, name, size, and SHA-256. The
  checksum is computed over the bytes received, never taken from the caller.
  `GET` on the same path streams the stored document back, so an approver can
  read what they are signing.
- `GET .../editions/{editionId}/blast-radius` returns the report: for a draft
  the effect of publishing it, for a published edition the effect of
  withdrawing it.
- `POST .../editions/{editionId}/publish` takes `{sourceDocument,
  appliesFrom, erratum?, erratumNote?}`. It returns 422 `errors.approver` when
  the approver is the curator, 422 `errors.evidence` with no evidence file, 422
  `errors.sourceDocument` or `errors.appliesFrom` when either is missing, and
  409 listing the rules when validation fails.
- `POST .../editions/{editionId}/withdraw` takes `{reason}`, 422
  `errors.reason` under 10 characters.
- `GET .../editions/{editionId}/changes` returns the change log the edition
  froze at publication, and `GET .../editions/{editionId}/events` the
  publication trail. Both are empty on a draft.

Errors use the module's shapes: `GhgFieldException` for 422 with an `errors`
map, `GhgRuleViolationException` for 409, `GhgNotFoundException.pack` for
404.

An import names a published edition and only a published one:
`POST /api/ghg/organizations/{id}/factor-packs/{packId}/import` answers 404 for
a superseded or withdrawn edition, which is what "readable, not importable"
means in the lifecycle table.

`GET /api/ghg/factor-packs` and `GET /api/ghg/factor-packs/{packId}` keep
their paths and response shapes. The widened `PackFactor` is internal: the
controller answers with its own records, and a factor still reads with the
one `sourceDetail` path it has always had, the three columns rejoined. The
two responses are byte for byte what they were before the catalogue. They list published editions only, so a
draft is never visible to an organization. A superseded edition stays
readable by identifier, so a past import can still be explained.

## Data

`V42__factor_pack_editions.sql` creates the catalogue and
`V43__seed_factor_pack_editions.sql` seeds the ten editions.
`V45__factor_pack_notices.sql` adds the notices publication raises (spec 02.7),
widens `ghg_emission_factors.source` and `ghg_run_factors.source` to
`varchar(2000)`, and adds the two audit actions spec 02.7 uses. Numbers assigned
in the planned implementation order; renumber if that order changes.

- `ghg_factor_packs` holds the families, keyed by `pack_key`, with the kind
  checked against `SOURCE` and `SECTOR`.
- `ghg_factor_pack_editions` holds the editions, with a unique `edition_id`,
  the `status` checked against the four values, the publication and evidence
  columns, `applies_from`, `supersedes_id`, the erratum flag and note, the
  curator and approver with the email each held, the withdrawal columns, and
  `provenance_review` checked against `REVIEWED` and `SEED_UNCHECKED`.
  Two checks carry rules of this spec: a published edition has a published
  moment, an evidence checksum, an applies-from date, a source document, and
  an approver unless `provenance_review` is `SEED_UNCHECKED`, which only
  `V43` sets; and the curator differs from the approver whenever both are
  present. A third refuses a withdrawal with no moment and no reason. The
  `edition_id` is the primary key rather than a surrogate, because it is the
  citation a report prints and it never changes, and a `provenance_note`
  column carries what the review covered, which the ten seeded editions use
  to say that their checksum is over the shipped JSON. The curator and the
  approver each record a name beside the user identifier and the email, so a
  seeded edition can read as the seed without inventing an account. The exemption exists for the ten seeded editions alone, whose
  values were already in production before the catalogue existed, and an
  edition authored in the console can never carry it.
- `ghg_factor_pack_rows` holds a row per factor per edition, unique on the
  edition and the code, carrying the columns `EmissionFactor` already
  carries plus `source_category`, `source_activity`, `source_detail`,
  `co2e_only`, and an `ordinal` that keeps the order the publication lists
  the rows in. The values are unconstrained `numeric`: a row states the
  publisher's figure at the scale the publication used, and the rounding to
  `numeric(12,6)` happens on import, where it happens today. A gas column is
  null where the publication states nothing, which is not the same as a
  stated zero.
- `ghg_factor_pack_changes` holds the change log frozen at publication, one
  row per code with the kind checked against `ADDED`, `CHANGED`,
  `DISCONTINUED`, and `UNCHANGED`, the old and new CO2e, the percent change,
  and the fields that moved.
- `ghg_factor_pack_events` holds the publication trail: the edition, the
  action, the actor with the email held, a detail, and the moment.

`ghg_emission_factors` gains `source_category`, `source_activity`, and
`source_detail`, backfilled from the seeded rows by `pack_code`. A factor
with no matching row keeps them null, because a hand-entered factor has no
publisher taxonomy. The backfill runs in `V43`, after the rows it reads
exist; `V42` adds the columns, backfills `source_edition` from the pack tag a
factor already carries, and swaps the unique index for the one spec 02.6
names. Spec 02.6 adds the columns versioning needs.

Everything stays in the `public` schema. A separate schema was declined:
`ghg_emission_factors` is half tenant data, referencing `ghg_organizations`
and referenced by `ghg_assignments`, so the boundary would not be clean, and
a second schema would need Flyway configured for it and changes to
`scripts/wipe-railway-db.sh` and `scripts/copy-railway-env.sh`, which both
drop and recreate `public`. The consequence accepted is that a wipe or an
environment copy re-seeds the catalogue, which is harmless because `V43` is
deterministic.

The seed is a committed migration rather than a startup task because
`make db-reset`, `make db-wipe`, the Testcontainers suite, and a fresh
production all replay from `V1`, so only a migration guarantees identical
editions everywhere, and because Flyway checksums the file, which is the
strongest mechanical guarantee that a published edition's values are never
edited.

`FactorPacks` reads the database instead of the classpath, keeping its `Pack`
and `PackFactor` records so `FactorPackController` and the import of spec
02.6 move with it. That continuity covers the tenant-facing read path only.
`Pack` carries no status, applies-from, curator, or approver, so the admin
API of this spec uses its own response records rather than reusing it. It
gains `headers()`, a projection of the edition columns
with a counted row total, so listing packs does not materialize 2,836 rows.
Assembled packs and the header list are cached and invalidated after a
publish, supersede, or withdraw commits. The cache is per instance, which
assumes one backend instance per environment, as Railway runs today.

## Events

None across module boundaries. Publication writes `ghg_factor_pack_events`
rather than `ghg_audit_events`, because `chk_ghg_audit_events_subject`
requires an organization or an inventory on every audit event and an edition
belongs to neither. That constraint stays as it is. The tenant-side acts of
spec 02.7 do belong to an organization, so they use `GhgAuditEvent`.
`FactorPackAdminService` resolves a curator's and an approver's name through
the `user` module's public `UserDirectory`, as `SupportAccessService` does.

## Verification

`FactorPackAdminApiIntegrationTests`, the authoring half: a draft is created,
edited, and deleted; a draft clones a predecessor's rows and correcting the copy leaves
the published edition alone; a draft is invisible on the tenant pack list; a
non-administrator gets 403; a published edition's rows, metadata and identity
cannot be edited or deleted; the validation report names every broken rule; a
code outside the namespace pattern is refused while `GHANA:td-losses` passes; a
zero value is allowed only on an unapproved template naming the document; the
gas split must reconcile within one percent; and a code names one row of an
edition.

`FactorPackPublicationApiIntegrationTests`: the approver cannot be the
curator; publishing needs an evidence file with a checksum, a source document
and an applies-from date; publishing is refused while any rule is broken;
publishing freezes the edition and its rows forever; the change log is computed
against the predecessor; an erratum marks the predecessor superseded and flags
the error while leaving its values alone; the blast radius names every holder
and the tonnage it would move, flags the rows moving more than 5 percent, and
names the open drafts and the rows inside a locked period; publishing changes no
organization's numbers, asserted over every factor value and every run total;
publishing raises one open notice per holder and none for an organization
holding nothing; withdrawing shows its own blast radius, hides the edition from
new imports and leaves held data alone, closing its open notices; the
publication trail records the evidence, the publication and the supersession; a
draft that was never published can be deleted and a published edition cannot;
and a non-administrator can neither publish nor read the blast radius.

`SeededFactorPackEditionsTest`, which `FactorPacksTest` became when the
publication rules landed, reads the seeded database instead of the classpath,
keeping every assertion it had: the ten editions carry the row counts the JSON
carried, every row arrives with the values, provenance, citation and taxonomy
the JSON file states, and each edition is published with an evidence checksum.
It adds the guard the rules needed: all ten pass every rule, not one row relies
on an exemption now that `V45` widened the citation column (the three long
citations are named with their lengths, so a change to them is noticed), and
`SEED_UNCHECKED` belongs to the ten and to nothing else, so an edition authored
in the console can never be published without an approver.

## Non-goals and open questions

- Versioned import, lineage identity, conflicts, locked periods, and
  retirement: spec 02.6. The notice, the diff, the named decision, and the
  recalculation question: spec 02.7.
- Automatic vintage updates stay a non-goal, as spec 02.1 records. A new
  edition is published and organizations decide.
- Supplier-specific factors never enter a shared pack. Chapter 6 ranks them
  ahead of a published table for the client that holds them, so they stay
  that organization's own factors with the supplier's document attached.
- Merging factors across organizations stays decided against in spec 02.3.
- Editing the seeded shared library, where `organization_id` is null, still
  needs a migration. This spec governs the catalogue, not the library.
- A family whose editions come from different publishers. A family is the
  lineage of one publication, and nothing enforces that.
- Whether an approver must re-confirm when a draft changes after the blast
  radius was read. It is computed on demand, so a stale reading is possible.
- A data quality or pedigree attribute on a pack row, such as the score the
  Scope 3 Technical Guidance defines. Spec 04.4 scores an activity record,
  not the factor applied to it, and verifiers increasingly ask for both.
  Whether the attribute belongs on the row, the edition, or neither is open.
- A record of who checked each row's transcription against the source
  document, as distinct from the approver who signed the edition. An
  approver attests to the edition; nothing attests row by row, and for an
  edition of 1,868 rows those are different claims. Whether a per-row check
  is worth its cost is open.
