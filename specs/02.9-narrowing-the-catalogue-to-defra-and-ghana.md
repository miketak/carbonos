# 02.9: Narrowing the catalogue to DEFRA and Ghana

- **Status**: Implemented (2026-09-15)
- **Protocol**: Chapter 6 (factors appropriate to the source), Chapter 7
  (transparency about the factors used), Chapter 9 (reporting the source of
  each factor), ISO 14064-1 (the basis of every quantification is stated)
- **Owner**: Michael Takrama
- **Created**: 2026-09-15
- **Modules**: `ghg` (the seeded catalogue, `FactorPacks`, the shared library),
  `src/features/ghg` (emission factors page), `src/features/admin` (factor pack
  console)

## Problem

The server shipped ten importable packs: `defra-2026`, `epa-hub-2025`,
`ember-grid-2025`, `refrigerants-ar5`, `ipcc-2006-process`,
`nga-2024-explosives`, `ghana`, and three sector packs, `sector-mining`,
`sector-oil-and-gas` and `sector-construction`. Between them they held 2,836
rows.

Breadth was the right thing while the catalogue was being built. It is the
wrong thing to ship. Chapter 6 asks for a factor appropriate to the source, and
a preparer at a Ghanaian gold plant offered a UK figure, a US figure and an
Australian figure for one activity is being handed a question the product
should have answered for them. Every extra pack is another edition to keep
current, another citation to defend in a verification, and another proxy to
justify under Chapter 7. Several packs were also thin: `nga-2024-explosives`
carried a single row, and `ipcc-2006-process` five.

The sector packs made it worse rather than better. They were selections that
re-cited EPA Hub, IPCC and NGA rows, so the same figure reached an organization
by more than one route, and the pack a factor arrived by was never the
publication it came from.

## Behavior

- **Two packs, and only two.** The catalogue offers `defra-2026` (UK DESNZ
  conversion factors 2026, 1,868 rows) and `ghana` (the Ember grid intensity by
  data year and the derived transmission-loss factor, 7 rows). Nothing else
  ships. `GET /ghg/factor-packs` returns two entries and the emission factors
  page shows two cards.
- **Eight families leave with their editions and rows.** `epa-hub`,
  `ember-grid`, `ipcc-process`, `nga-explosives`, `refrigerants`,
  `sector-construction`, `sector-mining` and `sector-oil-and-gas` are removed
  from the catalogue, along with the 961 rows they held.
- **The shared library keeps only DESNZ and Ghana rows.** Nine of its 23 rows
  go: explosives detonation (Australian NGA), quicklime calcination (IPCC
  2006), district cooling (an Ecoriv assumption rather than a published
  factor), and the six rows for a gas emitted as itself (CH4 fossil, CH4
  biogenic, N2O, CO2, SF6, NF3), which existed for the sector packs' venting
  path. Fourteen remain.
- **Ember stays as a citation, not as a pack.** The Ghana grid rows are
  Ember-derived by design and, under spec 02.3, a row cites the publication it
  comes from rather than the pack that selects it. Dropping the `ember-grid`
  pack never rewrites where a figure came from.
- **The console stays open.** A maintainer can still author a `defra-2027`, a
  new `ghana` edition or a family that does not exist yet, without a release
  (spec 02.5). `FactorPackKind.SECTOR` stays in the enum, unused by anything
  shipped. This spec narrows what CarbonOS stands behind, not what an
  administrator may curate.
- **A gas the Montreal Protocol covers is still reported outside the scopes.**
  DESNZ 2026 carries all twenty Montreal Protocol rows at
  `OUTSIDE_SCOPES_NON_KYOTO`, including HCFC-22, so the behaviour spec 02.4
  built survives the removal of the refrigerants pack that also carried them.

Given an organization with no factors: when it imports `defra-2026` and
`ghana`, then it holds 1,875 factors, the derived Ghana T&D loss row arrives
unapproved, and every row cites its own publication with its own years.

Given a preparer classifying a process emission: when they open the picker,
then no pack row is offered, because neither publication publishes one. They
enter the supplier or study factor by hand and approve it, which is what
Chapter 6 asks of a source the publications do not cover.

## API

Unchanged. Every endpoint of specs 02.1, 02.5, 02.6, 02.7 and 02.8 keeps its
shape; only what the catalogue contains changes. `packKey` and `editionId` stay
free-form strings, and no enum anywhere lists factor sources.

## Data

One migration, `V50__narrow_the_catalogue_to_defra_and_ghana.sql`. It inserts
nothing. `V43` is applied and Flyway checksums it, so the seed is never edited.

1. `ghg_factor_pack_notices` rows for a removed edition go first: they key on an
   edition and do not cascade.
2. `ghg_factor_pack_editions` and then `ghg_factor_packs`
   `WHERE pack_key NOT IN ('defra', 'ghana')`. Rows, changes and events cascade.
3. `ghg_emission_factor_packs` entries naming a removed edition are swept: the
   column is a plain varchar with no key into the catalogue.
4. A surviving factor whose `pack` or `source_edition` named a removed edition
   is repointed to a tag it still carries.
5. Organization factors that only a removed pack ever delivered are deleted,
   with their assignments released and their upstream rules removed. A factor a
   surviving pack also delivered stays: under spec 02.3 it is one factor.
6. The nine shared library rows are deleted.

A run line snapshots its factor's name, unit and value, and its `factor_id`
carries no foreign key, so a published report survives the removal untouched.
Nothing in the database would enforce that, so the migration refuses instead:
it raises if a run line cites a factor it would remove, and the environment is
wiped rather than quietly rewritten.

## Events

None. No domain event is published or consumed by this change.

## Verification

- `SeededFactorPackEditionsTest` asserts the catalogue holds exactly
  `defra-2026` and `ghana`, 1,875 rows between them, that both pass every
  publication rule of spec 02.5, and that the DESNZ Montreal Protocol rows
  still report outside the scopes.
- `GhgApiIntegrationTests` covers importing both packs, the unapproved Ghana
  T&D row, a Ghana row citing Ember rather than the Ghana pack, and a Montreal
  Protocol gas disclosed outside the scopes from DESNZ.
- `FactorPackImportIntegrationTests.aSecondPackSelectingTheSameRowTagsItRatherThanCopyingIt`
  keeps spec 02.3's one-row-many-tags behaviour covered. No two shipped packs
  share a code any more, so it builds two editions that do.
- The migration was replayed on a copy of a seeded database: 10 families to 2,
  10 editions to 2, 2,836 rows to 1,875, 23 library rows to 14; a factor
  delivered by both a removed and a surviving pack survived and was repointed;
  and the guard refused a database whose run line cited a doomed factor.
- Manual: `docs/qa/002-organization-setup.md` section F and
  `docs/qa/010-factor-pack-maintenance.md`.

## Non-goals and open questions

- **No process-emission factor ships any more.** All sixteen pack rows in that
  category were in removed packs, and the two library rows go with them.
  `ActivityCategory.PROCESS_EMISSIONS` and `StreamKind.PROCESS` stay, so an
  organization enters its own factor and a curator can author one. Restoring a
  published default is an authoring job, not an engineering one.
- **A gas recorded as itself no longer has a factor.** Deriving a vented or
  fugitive mass from a site gas composition was the oil and gas pack's path and
  left with it. The arithmetic (`GwpSet` applied per gas) is untouched and a
  hand-entered factor still uses it.
- **Blend compositions no longer arrive from a pack.** DESNZ publishes its
  refrigerant blends as a CO2e figure with the gas mass beside it and no
  composition, so a pack-delivered blend no longer reconverts under the
  inventory's GWP set. The shared library's R-410A row kept its composition
  until spec 02.10 retired the library; a hand-entered factor may carry one.
- Whether a curated Ghana or West Africa sector selection returns, authored in
  the console rather than seeded, is left open.
