# 02.3: Factor identity across packs

- **Status**: Implemented (2026-09-12)
- **Protocol**: Chapter 1 (consistency and transparency), Chapter 7
  (inventory quality management: one factor set, controlled), Chapter 9
  (the source of each emission factor is documented)
- **Owner**: Michael Takrama
- **Created**: 2026-09-12 (audit 2026-09-11, findings F22 and F23)
- **Modules**: `ghg` (`EmissionFactor`, `FactorPacks`, pack import, the
  factor endpoints, `GhgRunFactor`, the report and PDF factor table),
  `src/features/ghg` (emission factors page, classification picker),
  `scripts/gen-factor-packs.py`

## Problem

Spec 02.1 made the library extensible with packs, and a sector pack is a
selection of rows from the published packs. Two defects follow from how the
selection is stored. First, a factor imported through a sector pack cites
the pack, not the publication: every such row reads "Selection from the
DEFRA 2026, EPA Hub 2025, IPCC 2006 and NGA 2024 packs: ..." as its source
and carries the pack's year (2026) as its publication year, so an IPCC 2006
lime row prints "published 2026, data year 2006" in the report's factor
table and in the PDF. Chapter 9 requires the source of each factor; a
selection from four packs is not a citation, and a wrong publication year
defeats the vintage check a verifier runs on every factor. Second, the same
published row imported by two packs becomes two organization factors. After
five packs Asante Gold Resources had 182 factors: "Refrigerant R-410A
leakage (/kg)" three times, every Ghana grid year twice, "T&D losses, Ghana
(derived)" three times with different approval states, and a classification
list of 107 options with six identical labels. Which copy a preparer picked
is invisible in the report, and two sites can use two copies of "the same"
factor whose approval or later update differs, against the consistency
principle.

## Behavior

- **Every factor cites its publication.** A factor row carries the
  publication it comes from, whatever delivered it: **source** (the
  publication and the row within it, for example "UK Government (DESNZ) GHG
  conversion factors 2026, Fuels, Liquid fuels: Diesel (100% mineral
  diesel), per litre"), **source URL**, **publication year** and **data
  year** of that publication. A sector pack is a selection: importing it
  stores each factor with the provenance of the underlying publication, not
  of the pack. The pack level keeps its own description, licence and
  retrieval date for the pack card only.
- **Pack tags.** The pack or packs a factor was delivered by are stored
  apart from its provenance, as **tags**. A factor can carry several tags
  (`refrigerants-ar5`, `sector-mining`). Tags are shown on the emission
  factors page, in the classification picker and in the report's factor
  table, always separate from the source.
- **One factor per publication row.** Within an organization the
  publication row identifier (the pack code, for example `EPA:GWP:R-410A`,
  which every pack that selects the row already shares) is the identity of
  a factor. Importing a pack that contains a row the organization already
  holds adds the pack's tag to the existing factor and refreshes its values
  from the pack, as a re-import of the same pack does today. It never
  creates a copy. Approval is a property of the one factor: a pack that
  marks a row as needing review does not unapprove a factor another import
  approved, and a pack that approves a row does not approve a factor a user
  unapproved. Hand-entered factors (no pack code) are outside this rule and
  stay as they are.
- **Existing copies are merged.** A migration merges organization factors
  that share a pack code: the oldest approved copy survives (the oldest
  copy when none is approved), every assignment that pointed at a merged
  copy points at the survivor, the survivor's tags are the union of the
  copies' packs, and the copies are deleted. Runs are unaffected: run lines
  and run factors are snapshots that keep the id, name, values and source
  of whatever factor the run applied (`ghg_run_lines.factor_id` and
  `ghg_run_factors.factor_id` are plain identifiers with no foreign key),
  so a past report still names the copy it used. This migration rewrites
  data. Before it is deployed it is replayed with `psql` on a copy of a
  seeded database and the assignment counts are checked before and after,
  as `docs/how-to/add-a-migration.md` requires of a migration that rewrites
  existing data.
- **The classification picker.** The picker groups its options: the
  organization's own factors first, then the shared library. Each option
  shows the factor name and unit, then the publication and its year and the
  pack tags in a muted line, and an **unapproved** mark where it applies.
  Unapproved factors are hidden by default; a toggle **Show unapproved**
  reveals them, so a preparer cannot pick one without seeing that it is
  unapproved (the EMISSION_FACTOR gate still blocks the run). The picker is
  searchable by name, publication and tag. Two options never carry the
  same label: when two factors share a name and unit, the publication line
  tells them apart. Spec 05.5 reuses this picker for the paginated review.
- **The report and the PDF.** Section 08's factor table and the PDF's
  "Methodology and emission factors" table print the publication as the
  source, with its year, and the pack tags in their own column. Run factors
  snapshot the publication year, data year and tags at launch, so the table
  reads the same after the library changes.
- **Sector packs cite the publication.** `scripts/gen-factor-packs.py`
  emits, on every factor row of every pack, `sourcePublication`,
  `sourceUrl`, `publicationYear` and `dataYear` taken from the pack the
  row originates in (DEFRA, EPA Hub, Ember, IPCC, NGA, Ghana). The importer
  uses the row's provenance when present and the pack's otherwise, so an
  old pack file still imports. The three sector packs keep a pack-level
  `source` that describes the selection, for the card only.

Given Asante Gold Resources importing `refrigerants-ar5`, `defra-2026`,
`ghana`, `sector-mining` and `sector-oil-and-gas`: the organization holds
each publication row once; "Refrigerant R-410A leakage (/kg)" appears once,
tagged `refrigerants-ar5`, `sector-mining` and `sector-oil-and-gas`, with
"US EPA GHG Emission Factors Hub 2025, Tables 11-12 (IPCC AR5 GWP100),
published 2025" as its source; the IPCC lime row reads "published 2006,
data year 2006"; the picker for a kilogram record lists R-410A once and no
two options read the same.

## API

- `EmissionFactorResponse.pack` and `packCode` stay; `pack` becomes the
  first tag for compatibility and `packs[]` carries every tag.
- `POST /organizations/{id}/factor-packs/{packId}/import` → `{created,
  updated, tagged}`: `tagged` counts rows that already existed from another
  pack and only gained the tag.
- `GET /organizations/{id}/emission-factors` gains `?includeUnapproved=`
  (default `true` for the page) and `?q=` (matches name, source and tag).
  Spec 05.5 fetches the factor list once per page load and shares it with
  every row, so the picker applies the same two filters to that one list in
  the browser rather than refetching per row and per keystroke; the
  parameters serve any caller that wants the filtering done server-side.
- `ReportResponse.factors[]` and the run factor gain `publicationYear`,
  `dataYear` and `packs[]`.
- Unchanged: 409 to edit or delete a library factor; 409 to delete a
  factor a run applied.

## Data

`V38__factor_identity_across_packs.sql` (Number assigned in the planned
implementation order of the audit backlog; renumber if that order changes.):

- `ghg_emission_factor_packs (factor_id uuid references ghg_emission_factors
  on delete cascade, pack varchar(60), primary key (factor_id, pack))`,
  backfilled from `pack`. A join table rather than an array column: it is
  queryable ("which factors did `sector-mining` deliver"), it keeps the
  uniqueness rule a plain constraint, and Hibernate's schema validation
  handles it without a custom type.
- The merge described in Behavior, in SQL, with `ghg_assignments.
  emission_factor_id` re-pointed before the copies are deleted.
- `idx_ghg_emission_factors_pack_code` becomes unique on
  `(organization_id, pack_code) WHERE pack_code IS NOT NULL`; the `pack`
  column stays as the first tag.
- `publication_year`, `data_year` and `packs` (text, comma-separated tags
  as snapshotted) on `ghg_run_factors`, null for runs before the migration.
- The pack JSON files regenerated with row-level provenance.

## Verification

`GhgApiIntegrationTests.aPublicationRowIsOneFactorWhateverPackDeliversIt`
(the second pack tags instead of copying; the count of factors after five
packs equals the count of distinct pack codes; a sector-pack factor cites
its publication and year; approval survives a re-import; the report's
factor table prints publication and tags). A migration test on a seeded
copy: two copies of `EPA:GWP:R-410A` with one assignment each merge into
one factor with two assignments and two tags, and a run that applied a
merged copy still prints its snapshot. Frontend:
`InventoryDetailPage.test.tsx` (grouped picker, hidden unapproved rows
and the toggle, search, publication line), `EmissionFactorsPage.test.tsx`
(tags shown apart from the source).

## Non-goals and open questions

- Merging factors across organizations, or between an organization and the
  shared library: a library row and an imported row with the same
  publication stay distinct, as spec 02.1 decided.
- Re-pointing run snapshots at the survivor: snapshots are historical and
  keep the id they used.
- A pack that changes the publication row it selects between editions gets
  a new pack code; nothing merges across editions.
- Whether a user-entered factor can be given a pack code by hand to adopt
  a publication row's identity. Open; not needed for the audit findings.
- The picker's grouping and filtering run in the browser over the single
  factor list spec 05.5 fetches per page; the API parameters exist and are
  covered by `GhgApiIntegrationTests`, but the page does not call them.
